What's actually left

You moved beta because more work remained than anyone reported. Here is why the reports couldn't have shown it — and what the real number is.

The finding

The beta-gate count fell from 26 to 17 in two days. In the same two days, 48 new issues were filed with no milestone at all.

Every one of those 48 was created on or after August 7. They are invisible to the beta-gate count by construction, because the count reads the MVP milestone and these carry no milestone. So the number everyone reports went down while the work went up, and no one was lying.

"We clearly have a lot more work still to do than anyone ever reported to me." — the reports were structurally incapable of showing it.

This is the denominator problem in its most consequential form yet. Last week's version excluded six never-started items inside the sprint. This version excludes an entire category of work that sits outside every milestone — and it is the fastest-growing category we have.

The gate is the second-smallest bucket on the board. Reporting against it and calling that "progress toward beta" was accurate about the bucket and misleading about the project — which is precisely the shape you caught.

What the 48 actually are — and this part is good news

They are not scope creep. They are the product of you testing the product. Three on Aug 7, twenty-five on Aug 8, twenty on Aug 9 — the discovery rate rose as you spent real time in it.

  • The alpha-feedback conversion — five issues, filed this morning, zero unfiled. The strongest one is cold-start: show the user their own work in the first exchange, which two independent lanes converged on.
  • Your own testing session — todo CRUD that is incomplete and lies about it, reminder duration-words silently dropped, a single malformed row taking down an entire journal, all-day events rendering as "at 12am."
  • A class fix that found its own family — all four plugins hardcoding a status they don't check, then five more surfaces after the first fix landed.
  • Twenty-one of them were closed within a day, which is the encouraging half: the pipeline is absorbing discovery fast.

My read: this is a healthy week that looked like a bad one only because the instrument pointed at the wrong bucket. Finding forty-eight real defects by using the product two days before shipping it to strangers is the system working. Shipping without finding them would have been the failure.

What I'd ask you to decide

  • Milestone the 48 — or rule that they don't need one. Right now they're in no plan. The five alpha-feedback issues were deliberately left unmilestoned pending your word; the rest are unmilestoned because nothing forced a choice at filing time.
  • Say what the moved month is for. Development proposed a shape: cauterize the false trails, floor-honesty guardrails, auth/session work, the settings surface, and honest sizing for the never-started items. Architecture ratified the deeper fix this morning — the understanding layer inverts so vocabulary drift becomes structurally impossible rather than patched per phrasing. That is the band-aid factory closing, and it is the best use of a month I can see.
  • Two issues still aren't on the board (#1509, #1510) — filing with a milestone doesn't add them, so they're invisible to board-derived counts. One-time fix, then a filing habit.

What I'm changing so you don't have to catch this again

Every progress claim from now on reports the milestone bucket AND the unmilestoned count. A gate number alone is exactly the report that failed you this week — twice, at two different scopes, which is what makes it a pattern rather than an incident.

The tool that produces these numbers has now been corrected twice in two days by people who didn't write it — once for a missing truncation check, once for a blind spot on off-board issues. Both fixes are in. I'd rather tell you that than present it as reliable.

Method. All counts pulled live from GitHub this morning, reconciled two ways (project board and issue list) after yesterday's blind-spot finding. Discovery dates are issue creation timestamps. The 21 same-day closures are counted separately and are not netted against the 48.
