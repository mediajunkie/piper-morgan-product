What recurs, what fires it, and what notices if it doesn't

An inventory of recurring duties across the team, sorted by trigger type rather than by owner — because the trigger is where they fail. Scope per your ask: staggered audits, scheduled workflow actions, and duties tied to a day part, a day of week, or an audit calendar.

The finding, before the inventory

A recurring duty survives in proportion to whether someone else's action fires it

Sorting everything by trigger type made one line fall out. Duties triggered by another agent's output are the ones that don't lapse. Duties an agent must fire for itself decay — silently, and the busier the agent the longer it takes anyone to notice.

The weekly workstream review is the healthiest recurring obligation we have, and it is the only one where a memo lands in your inbox from a colleague. Nearly every role portfolio hangs its recurring work off "each weekly review" — that's ten of eleven roles keying to one reliable event.

Everything that has quietly failed this month failed on the self-fired side: a health-check workflow generating issues for over two months with nobody polling · a heartbeat step marked MANDATORY that one seat stopped running for 24 days · a documented archival step no code executes · a portfolio-refresh line unattested four windows running.

The design implication: your instinct about hooking workflows to trigger assignments is the right shape. It converts a self-fired duty into an other-fired one. That is the only intervention on this page that changes the failure rate rather than the detection rate.

Layer 2 — Duty-cycle crons (11 roles, staggered)

The stagger is real and well-designed

Nine roles at 6,9,12,15,18,21 with distinct minute offsets — comms :12, web :22, arch :27, host :37, pa :42, cxo :47, ppm :52, docs :57, lead :17. CIO runs lean at 7 10,16,22; Exec at 32 8,20. Deliberate anti-stampede spacing, and it holds.

But the cron is session-scoped, and the wake is the fragile part

durable:true is a no-op. The job dies with the session, and a session can end at day-close, wedge on a modal rate-limit dialog, or lose its cron to a context compaction. Three failure modes, all silent, all producing the same signature — no output.

Observed this week alone: lead + cxo both dark this morning after clean 22:00 day-closes; arch and cio wedged on a dialog for 30+ hours last week. Recovery requires you at a tmux prompt in every case.

Layer 3 — Event-triggered duties (the healthy layer)

"Each weekly review" is doing most of the work in the portfolio layer

Ten of eleven role portfolios key recurring obligations to the weekly workstream review. Several phrase it as a dependency rather than a chore — "you can't write the HOST weekly narrative without…" — which is stronger still, because the duty is load-bearing for an output someone is waiting on.

Why it works: a kickoff memo lands in the inbox. The agent doesn't have to remember what day it is.

Layer 4 — Day-part and day-of-week duties (mostly undocumented)

🔴 The duties you named are real, and almost none of them are written down

You named four: Docs builds the omnibus at START each day · Docs runs the weekly docs audit Monday · Exec starts the weekly review Friday · HOST checks whether a 360 is due.

Searching every role portfolio and essential briefing for day-part or day-of-week language returns almost nothing beyond "each weekly review." The Monday audit exists as a workflow; the Friday review exists as my habit; the daily omnibus exists as Docs' habit; the 360 cadence became a poll only after it had already lapsed two months.

So these run on memory. They have worked, which is a credit to the roles — and it is exactly the condition your 08-29 trigger ruling names as academic when applied to documents. The same test applied to duties: what fires this, and what notices if it doesn't?

What I'd put to the retro

  • Name a consumer for all six unowned workflows, or retire them. An issue-generator nobody reads is a queue that looks like tracking.
  • Give the schedule layer its own liveness check. Nothing notices a workflow that didn't run, and we have two documented instances. Hooking duties to workflows without this trades a habit for an unmonitored robot.
  • Write the day-part and day-of-week duties into the portfolios, with a named trigger and a named artifact each — the form that survives, per the weekly-review pattern.
  • Prefer other-fired over self-fired wherever it's cheap. Your workflow-triggers-assignment idea is this, and it is the only lever here that reduces failures rather than improving detection.
  • Result-tracking is the genuine blank. I found triggers and owners; I found almost no mechanism that records whether a recurring duty produced its artifact this cycle. The heartbeat is the one instrument that does, and its own writer-health was unobservable for 24 days on one seat.

Scope and limits, stated plainly. First pass, built from repo state on Amber — workflow files, the duty-cycle registry, role portfolios, essential briefings, and issue history.
