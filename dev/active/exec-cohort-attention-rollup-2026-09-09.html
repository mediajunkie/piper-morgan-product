🟡 The fresh insight, and it changes what the fix has to be

A pull that waits for idle cannot drain a backlog in a cohort this active. Lead is the only builder among eleven roles, so Lead is also the busiest recipient. The conditions under which the intake step runs are the conditions Lead least often reaches.

The mechanism isn't wrong — it's subordinate. Everything inbound outranks it by construction, and inbound is generated by ten colleagues who are all working well.

What would actually change the number: the backlog item competes with inbound rather than waiting for its absence — e.g. one claimed item held open across fires, advanced whenever a fire has room, rather than started only when a fire has nothing else. That is a different rule from the one you approved, and it's Arch's and CIO's to design, not mine to assert.

⚠️ Stated as a hypothesis: I have one day of evidence and a directive that has not yet been exercised. It may simply need a quiet day to prove itself. The falsifiable test is cheap — watch for the first quiet Lead fire and see whether an item gets claimed. If one happens and nothing is claimed, the rule is wrong. If no quiet fire happens all week, the rule is unreachable, which is the same problem by a different route.
