The contributor experiment — your idea, first run

All four filed, on the day they were asked, with no milestone apparatus required. A better response rate than the leadership tier, from roles that had never been asked before — and the reports were not thin.

  • Development — the build-complete report above, plus a regression they own outright: an unescaped apostrophe in the honesty-copy work silently killed the chat history renderer. It passed green CI because nothing parses template-embedded JavaScript. Gap named and pinned.
  • Documentation — closed a six-day log gap with parallel extraction, then found two independent bugs in a housekeeping workflow that had likely never successfully run since it was written.
  • Product assistant — found the deployment gap and sent it URGENT two days before target. Their headline number was wrong by two orders of magnitude; they say so plainly, and note the underlying finding held and was independently reconfirmed.
  • Web — fixed a live production bug where the blog returned HTTP 200 with a not-found body for any bad slug: a soft 404, invisible to status-code checks. Also discovered the role was missing from the org roster entirely, not just missing a briefing.

My read: keep it. The engineering, publishing, and web lanes were invisible to this cycle a week ago. Three of the four surfaced defects that no leadership lane would have seen, because they're found by doing the work rather than reviewing it.

The pattern across all ten reports

Every significant correction this week came from someone other than the author — and none of it is mechanized.

Innovation put it as the sharpest version of their open question to you: "every correction that mattered came from someone other than the author. None of it is mechanized." The evidence is unusually clean this week because the reports volunteer it:

  • Experience's "shipped" claim was corrected by extending someone else's deployment check into their own lane.
  • The product assistant's headline number was corrected by development, and the correction then traveled to two more roles who had made the identical error.
  • Innovation credited a fix to themselves that trust had proposed six days earlier — and reported that against themselves.
  • Trust found their own framework lapsed across four reviews, using a checker built with experience.
  • Architecture marks their own ratchet unattested rather than assume it.

What I'd put in front of you: this is a genuine capability and it is currently a culture rather than a mechanism. It survives on eleven roles independently choosing to check each other and to report against themselves. It is also the thing most likely to erode quietly as everyone gets busier and more self-sufficient — which is precisely innovation's question, and I'd answer it yes.
