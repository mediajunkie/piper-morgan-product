### ⚠️ Step 0 self-heal — and the check that was supposed to detect it gave a FALSE PASS

Prior day (7/29) **did not STOP** — PM signed off for the night and no close ran. Retroactive close now
written into `dev/2026/07/29/2026-07-29-0939-cxo-code-log.md` (day arc, what I got wrong, memory-eval
3-bucket, sign-off checklist, real `<!-- DAY-CLOSED: 2026-07-29 -->` sentinel).

**But the skill's own detector said the day was closed.** Step 0 specifies:

```
grep -l "DAY-CLOSED" dev/2026/<prior-day-path>/*{role}*log.md
```

That returned my 7/29 log — **matching prose, not a sentinel.** Line 100 of that log reads
*"**4. Retroactive `DAY-CLOSED`** on the Jul 19 log, per Exec's kickoff ask…"* — i.e. the log was
describing a close it performed **for a different day**. The bare-substring grep can't tell the
difference between *having* a marker and *talking about* markers.

**The failure is systematic, not a fluke**: the logs most likely to discuss `DAY-CLOSED` in prose are
precisely the ones performing a retroactive close for another day — which is exactly what a
self-healing agent writes. **So the self-heal step defeats its own detector on the days it does its
job.** Mine did it inside 24 hours of my writing the Jul-19 close.

Had I trusted it, 7/29 would have stayed silently open — and the log would have *looked* closed to
every future check, including Docs's merge-keeper sweep if it greps the same way.

**Fix is one anchored pattern**: `grep -lE '^<!-- DAY-CLOSED: [0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2} -->' …`
(line-anchored, date-shaped). Reporting to CIO this fire. Same family as this week's running theme —
**a check that returns a confident pass while measuring the wrong thing.**
