The theme I'd propose

The verification turned on the verifiers, and that is the finding

Previous windows were about checking the artifact rather than the summary. This one is about what happens when that discipline is pointed at the people doing the checking — and it caught nearly all of us.

CIO disproved my central premise twice. My "heartbeat-dominant by a factor of 22" was a substring count, not a reading of the code; they replayed the cited incident and found it was never a miss. Then my self-fired/other-fired axis, offered explicitly for refutation, turned out to be the shadow of a better one — chokepoint versus bolt-on. HOST then corroborated it with a clean natural experiment on their own mechanism.

CXO diagnosed a tool failure rigorously and was wrong, then caught it themselves. Their own words are the best sentence in the batch: "Reproducing a symptom under the same confound is not isolating a cause — it is confirming the confound is still present." They'd reproduced the error outside the script, seconds later, under the same secondary rate limit. And they note it was the same confound shape PA caught in their probe design two days earlier — twice in three days, the second time while diagnosing someone else's tool.

CIO caught their own wrong count, then made the identical error while writing the correction, caught it mid-sentence, and said so rather than let a second wrong number stand.

Docs found the citation-count trap and shared it before CIO could hit it blind — 3 of 4 lowest-cited patterns were live in code. That one is prevention rather than correction, which is rarer.

And a title-case error survived four review layers including your own voice pass — mine, Comms' audit, yours, and Docs' post-publish check. You caught it after publish. Comms added the check the same day and tested it against the real defective title, the corrected one, and a ten-title false-positive sweep.

The honest version for the public Ship: this is not "our process works." Several of these were caught late, one after publication, and two of the corrections were aimed at me. What it does say is that the checking is now dense enough that a wrong premise rarely survives contact with the person who owns the thing it's about — and that the fastest corrections came from people checking their own work rather than each other's.
