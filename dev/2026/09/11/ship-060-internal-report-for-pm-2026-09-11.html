The week's three arcs

1. You named a feeling on Monday and it was ratified law by Friday

Your "something has been lost despite this huge autonomy improvement and I honestly don't understand it" → scope → Arch leading → seven independent reads returned same-day → synthesis of seven decisions → a challenge round producing five accepted amendments → your ratification this morning. Three working days, kickoff to canon.

The finding underneath it was concrete: the duty cycle had no work source except mail and standing-items. Nothing in the procedure read the sprint board. That is why "there is no work" and "35 never-started items" were simultaneously true, and why Lead's quiet fires were the procedure operating correctly.

Worth noting the challenge round caught the synthesis's own enforcement labels outrunning their evidence twice, and Arch withdrew their own proposed fold on its stated cost. A synthesis whose author's proposals all survived would be the one to distrust.

2. Your two test rounds produced the week's engineering spine

Four convergent bugs from your live testing became one contract. #1617, #1631, #1650 and #1694 were the same acceptance mechanism failing in opposite directions — a question with no affirmative token fired a state change, while a bare "yes" did nothing. Lead turned that into a single predicate (#1739) with an adoption ratchet: idea to full adoption in 48 hours, three issues closed together.

Security effectively closed in-window. #1734 (admin gate, hash-proven), #1732 (chat-render sanitizer — and in fixing it Lead found the cited file was an unserved twin plus a fourth sink nobody had listed), #1637 (six stale pins and a collection-time hazard).

The backlog got a shape. Arch's un-modeled-noun audit — 435 issues, 6 confirmed cousins, "a convention is not a model" — supplied the epic boundaries; PPM built the ordered artifact Lead now actually works from. 37 items → 8 dependency-ordered epics + 6 honest singletons.

3. The methodology corpus had its most productive week in months

Four entries filed — m-50 (self-attestation isn't verification), m-51 (a bounded search isn't a total), m-52 (open it), m-53 (chokepoint vs bolt-on) — each corroborated across multiple seats before filing. CIO's framing is the right one: an entry earns canon by surviving contact with more than one seat, not by one agent asserting it.

🔴 The honest counterweight

"Almost none of it is verified at the layer that matters. Every contract I shipped this week is checked at 'source and structure,' and not one has been scored against a delivered turn. That gap is my workstream's real status, and it did not close this week."

That generalises past CXO. A great deal of this week's output is contracts, predicates, rulesets and methodology — things verified at the layer where they are written, not the layer where a user meets them. Your live rounds are currently the only instrument that tests the second layer, and they found four real defects in twenty minutes each time.
