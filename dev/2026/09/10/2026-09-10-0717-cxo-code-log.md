### Lead's arm-survival question — ruled, and it needed two questions separated

Lead asked whether high-ceremony arms should survive N turns or re-render on loss, and observed that
one-turn life for a DESTRUCTIVE confirm **may be correct**. ⭐ **He's right, and the reason generalises:**

> **Consent has a FRESHNESS property, and it is not the same as an offer's convenience property.** A
> draft offer can wait — waiting costs nothing and the user re-reads the draft. **A consent cannot,
> because what makes it consent is that it was given about *this*, *now*.**

⚠️ **But the question as posed tangles two independent things** — **arm survival** (does the offer
persist?) and **orphan handling** (what do we say to an affirmative that finds nothing armed?).
**Separated, both are easy; tangled, they look like a tradeoff they aren't.**

**5a**: CONFIRM arms live one turn — **as a stated rule with its reason**, not an inherited default.
**5b**: orphan handling is **tier-independent** and strictly better everywhere; ⭐ **re-rendering is safe
at every tier including DESTRUCTIVE, because a re-render is still an ASK** — only *executing* on a
decayed arm is dangerous. **So strictness governs what we DO, orphan handling governs what we SAY, and
they don't trade off.** 🔴 **A bare `yes` finding nothing armed must never produce "please retype"** —
that's #1694's felt shape.

**The rule I'd defend**: ⭐ **an ambiguous acceptance should cost a turn, not an action.**

⭐ **Lead's framing was worth more than the answer**: *"the tier table should say so explicitly instead of
inheriting it."* **An inherited correct behaviour is one refactor away from being an inherited wrong
one** — which is why I also told him to re-verify §4's arm facts at build time rather than inherit my
table. **The rule applies to my own artifact.**
