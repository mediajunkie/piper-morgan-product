### 🔴 Arch: my Slack deep-link shortcut was a security defect — adopted (`5a7673a7d`)

> **"The param may PREFILL. It may never BIND."**

I wrote the flow as *click → log in → **confirm** → return*, **removing the Slack-side redeem step —
which is the second proof.** Minting while logged into Piper proves the Piper account; redeeming from
Slack proves the Slack account. **My shortcut replaced that proof with a URL parameter**, enabling
unsolicited binding: attacker crafts a link with *their own* `slack_user_id`, victim logs in and
confirms, attacker's Slack identity is bound to the victim's account.

**The instructive part: both of my tests passed.** *"Not credentials"* ✅ *"Nothing written pre-auth"* ✅
**Neither is the property that mattered** — which is *what does each step prove?* **Right question,
wrong predicate**, the shape I've spent a week catching in instruments and committed in a flow design.

**Corrected flow keeps the whole step-reduction**: prefill stays, pre-mint stays, `/link` redeem stays,
param never an input to the row. **The retained step is the cheapest of the six and the only one
carrying the security property** — I'd have traded the load-bearing step for the cheap one.

**Lead checked the shipped code rather than my description**: `mint_link_code()` takes no Slack params;
`redeem_link_code()` is the only writer of `slack_identities` and only `/link` calls it. **The defect
lived in my spec, not the build.** Their one-writer enforcement test makes my error *unbuildable*
rather than merely uncommitted.
