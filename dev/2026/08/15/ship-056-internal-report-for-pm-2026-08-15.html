The pattern across all ten reports

Every lane, without exception, reported catching a claim by checking the actual source instead of trusting a summary — including its own summaries.

This isn't one instrument's finding this week; it's the throughline of the entire report set. The evidence is unusually direct because every report volunteers its own instance:

  • Product audited a design proposal against the actual shipped code twice and found two real gaps neither author had seen — including one where a correct pattern (disclose-after for a low-stakes action) had been silently generalized to a case where it would have let a destructive action skip its required confirmation.
  • Communications traced a "six releases" claim in its own prior Ship review back to the actual deploy record and found it was one release, not six — caught by tracing the artifact, not by re-reading the prose.
  • The product assistant caught itself mid-verification citing a branch that was 18 days stale as "what's live," corrected the record, and wrote the trap into the project's own standing documentation so it can't recur unnoticed.
  • Trust found two real bugs in the tooling it built specifically to catch this class of error — in its own documents — by running its own checker rather than assuming a prior fix had held.
  • Architecture's own incident this window is the sharpest instance: a merge deleted 22 files because a remediation step was followed in the wrong direction, missed because the check that would have caught it measured presence, not direction.

What I'd put in front of you: the sprint's own denominator broke twice this week — once in a brand-new tool, once in board data — and both times the fix that landed was structural (a script, a checker, a re-derivation) rather than someone simply being more careful next time. That's the same shape as #054's "clear is not a measurement" and #055's "shipped is a layer word," landing a third time in a row. I'd read that as the team having actually internalized it rather than merely having named it well.
