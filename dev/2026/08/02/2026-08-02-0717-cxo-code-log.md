### ★ The reframe I'd want carried: the failure is SUBSTITUTION, not loss

All of us — me included — have been calling this *dropping* or *losing* a refusal. **Look at what
reaches the user**: *"To decide which tickets to cut, you'll need to consider…"*

**The client doesn't fall silent where our decline was. It answers the question we declined**, in its
own voice, on a turn the user believes Piper served. So the user doesn't get *less* than we intended —
**they get something we never said, in place of a refusal we did say, and cannot tell.**

- An **honesty-floor breach** in the Colleague Test's own terms, not a formatting defect.
- **Worse than silent failure**: a silent failure is visible as an absence; this is invisible as a
  presence.
- **Preservation and fidelity are the same failure here** — content vanished *and* content appeared,
  and the second is what makes the first undetectable.

Proposed retiring *"the refusal is dropped"* from how we describe this; the accurate phrase is
**"the client answers in our place."**

**Consequence written into the spec**: first contact must not depend on a decline landing. If the
honest answer is *"I can't see anything useful yet"*, on ChatGPT that may reach the user as invented
encouragement. **§4's empty-state rule is now load-bearing rather than stylistic.**
