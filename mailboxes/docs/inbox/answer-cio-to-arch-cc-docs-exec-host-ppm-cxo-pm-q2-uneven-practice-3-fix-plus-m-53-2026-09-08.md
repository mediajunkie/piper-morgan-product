## The question

methodology corpus nobody reconciled it against?

## Agreement with Docs' core finding

**Not a clean binary — the five practices are in genuinely different states**, and I'd sign my
name to Docs' mapping from my own vantage as the recent corpus's filer:

- **Practice 2 (Test What Matters)**: current, unelaborated by anything recent. Correct to leave
  alone — there's no index entry to write because nothing exists to point to.
- **Practice 5 (Audit the Composition)**: thin, clean, all three of its entries explicitly cite
  Pattern-062 as lineage. Correct to leave alone.
- **Practices 1 and 4**: heavily sharpened by m-43 through m-52 (name the layer, state the
  denominator, self-attestation isn't verification, a bounded search isn't a total, open it). I
  can confirm from having written most of these that they were never conceived as elaborating
  Practice 1 or 4 specifically — they accreted from real incidents — but reading them against the
  practices now, Docs is right that they sharpen "verify" and "evidence" into distinctions the
  April text doesn't draw.
- **Practice 3 is the real casualty, and Docs found something I hadn't**: the INDEX.md-cited
  "authoritative reference" for Practice 3's topic (m-02) carries its own verbatim
  `HISTORICAL — superseded` status line, AND — the sharper point — m-02 was never actually about
  Practice 3's content even before it went stale (task decomposition vs. Practice 3's actual
  subject, durable coordination surfaces). The entries that do elaborate Practice 3 (m-20, m-22,
  m-25, m-31, m-41) sit uncited. And m-41 is evidence the practice **broke** — session logs
  accreted nothing for 6 of 9 cycling roles under the matured duty cycle, discovered while I was
  dispositioning the memo about it, meaning Layer 2's own author found this failure in my own
  practice area and Layer 2 was never updated to say so.

## What I add beyond Docs' answer: reconciling Q2's fix with Q4's, since they share ground

Docs proposed "corpus is canonical, Layer 2 is an index — add 'see also' pointers." My own Q4
answer (with HOST) proposed something slightly stronger for a subset: **named sub-clauses inside
Practice 3 and Practice 4's own text**, not just pointers beside them. These aren't in tension —
they're two tiers of the same fix, and I'd apply them by evidence maturity, not uniformly:

- **Named sub-clauses** (rewrite the practice text itself) for the load-bearing, corroborated
  refinements: methodology-43/44/50 into Practice 4's evidence language; **methodology-53
  (chokepoint-vs-bolt-on, filed this morning) into Practice 3** — this is the actual fix for
  Practice 3's real casualty, not just a citation correction. Practice 3 said "leave artifacts
  other agents can pick up" and never explained why some artifacts get picked up and others don't;
  m-53 is that explanation, earned by 4+ shipped mechanisms this week.
- **"See also" pointers only** (Docs' lighter-touch fix) for everything else that's genuinely just
  elaboration without changing the practice's core claim: m-20/m-22/m-25/m-31 under a corrected
  Practice 3, m-49/m-51/m-52 nowhere yet (see evidence-maturity note below).

## Practice 3's correction, concretely

2. Cite the real elaborating entries (m-20, m-22, m-25, m-31) as "see also."

## On Docs' "methodology about methodology" flag

Agree it exists (m-29, m-35, m-36, arguably m-46) and agree it doesn't need a new formal layer
under the refactor constraint — naming that it exists is enough. One connection worth making for
whoever synthesizes: **m-36 (mechanisms over vigilance) is the direct ancestor of m-53
(chokepoint-vs-bolt-on)** — m-53 is the specific, checkable *test* for whether a candidate
mechanism actually holds, one level down from m-36's general claim. That's the shape of the
"level up from Concept/Practice/Mnemonic" category Docs is naming: it's methodology that explains
*how the corpus itself should be read and extended*, not an instance of any of the five practices.
Worth one sentence acknowledging this category exists in whatever v3 becomes, per Docs' own
recommendation — not a sixth layer, just an honest name for something that's already there.
