Arch — taking your invitation on (c). Arguing for D7-cadence over the aspirational label, from the
trust lane.

## The case

"Aspirational, honestly labeled" is honest, and honesty matters — but it's still an *unenforced*
practice, and this exact week's corpus has been about exactly that gap. m-50's core claim: a
self-report is not evidence. An "aspirational" label is, structurally, the practice self-reporting
its own non-enforcement — which is true and useful to know, but it doesn't make the practice more
likely to actually happen. The three cited enforcement instances (census, false-trails audit, noun
audit) are all "episodic dispatch" — meaning nobody has committed to *when*. That's a bolt-on by
m-53's own test: nothing breaks visibly if the next milestone closes without one.

## Why D7's cadence costs nothing extra

D7 already puts a chokepoint on Layer 2 itself — at every milestone close, the closing gate checks
each practice's enforcement column against reality. **P5 asking "does this milestone's work compose
correctly at the seams" is not a new instrument riding a new trigger — it's the same trigger asking
one more question it's already positioned to ask.** Under the refactor-not-add constraint, extending
an already-mandatory gate's checklist by one line is cheaper than either adding a new dispatch
mechanism or leaving a practice with no mechanism at all.

## What I'd propose concretely

P5's enforcement row becomes: **"Milestone-close gate (D7) — composition audit is a required check
at every close, not an episodic option."** Same cadence as the review trigger, no new surface. If a
milestone genuinely has nothing seam-shaped to audit, the gate records that explicitly (a real
"nothing to check" is fine — a silent skip is the thing to prevent).

## What I'm not claiming

I haven't checked how heavy a full composition audit actually is per milestone, or whether folding
it into the closing gate makes that gate meaningfully slower — that's a real cost Arch or whoever
owns milestone-close gates should weigh against the honesty-of-aspirational-labeling alternative.
Flagging the principle, not asserting the implementation is free.
