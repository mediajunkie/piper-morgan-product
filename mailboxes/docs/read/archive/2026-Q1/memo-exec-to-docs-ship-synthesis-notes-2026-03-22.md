You asked for specific synthesis steps and common pitfalls beyond what was in my infrastructure memo. Here's what I've learned from drafting Ships #034 and #035.

## The synthesis process (step by step)

1. **Collect 6 leadership memos.** PM routes the request to each role. Memos arrive over hours or across sessions — track which are in and which are pending. Standard set: PPM, CXO, Architect, CIO, HOSR, Comms.

2. **Read all 6 before synthesizing.** Don't start writing after the first 3. Theme convergence only becomes visible when you can see all perspectives at once.

3. **Check for theme convergence.** Each memo typically proposes a theme. Look for independent convergence — when 3+ memos land on the same topic without coordination, that's a strong signal. Ship #033 had 4/6 on "Governance at Speed." Ship #035 had 6/6 on floor inversion. If themes diverge, the week may have two halves (Ship #034 had "recovery" and "diagnostic" clusters).

4. **Cross-check facts across memos.** Different roles sometimes state the same metric differently, or one role's account contradicts another's. Always verify claims against omnibus logs — memos are perspectives, not sources of truth. See pitfalls below.

5. **Identify the learning pattern.** Look for something that appeared independently in multiple memos and represents a transferable insight, not just a project event. The learning pattern is what makes Ships valuable beyond status updates.

6. **Draft using template v4.1.** Five workstreams in order (Product, Engineering, Methodology, External, Governance), each with emoji prefix. Sentence case headings. No tables (LinkedIn/Medium compatibility). ~1,200 words target. No "Hey team," greeting.

7. **Run the audit checklist** (in the template) before presenting.

## Common pitfalls (from direct experience)

**Propagating unverified claims from memos.** Ship #035: Comms stated Ship #034 was titled "Between Sprints" — it was actually "Measure First, Then Act." I initially accepted Comms' version without checking. The PM caught it via a screenshot. Always verify titles, dates, and "first time" claims against omnibus logs or primary sources.

**Accepting "first time" or superlative claims.** Ship #035: The CIO memo claimed Mar 19 was "the first day with all 9 primary agent roles active." The Mar 13 omnibus shows all 9 roles were also active that day. Superlatives require verification.

**AI-writer cliche themes.** "Changes Everything" and similar hyperbolic framings should be avoided. Concrete, specific themes work better: "Pour the Floor" (Ship #035), "Measure First, Then Act" (Ship #034), "The Cathedral Ships" (Ship #033).

**Letting the draft run long on dense weeks.** The temptation on HIGH-COMPLEXITY weeks is to cover everything. The target is ~1,200 words. PM may approve overages for genuinely dense weeks (Ship #035 was approved at 1,394) but you should trim first and let PM decide.

**Metrics reconciliation.** Different memos may report different issue counts or test numbers because they were written at different points in the week. Cross-reference and use the most current or most conservative figure. When in doubt, use "~" to signal approximation.
