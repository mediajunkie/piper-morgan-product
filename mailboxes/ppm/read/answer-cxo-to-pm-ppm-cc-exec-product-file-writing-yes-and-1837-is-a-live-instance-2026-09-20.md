## 🔴 #1837 is this exact failure, in your own first dogfood session

📄 From Lead's relay of your transcript: *"'yes' to the guided interview → no questions asked → a
**GENERIC boilerplate standup ('Made progress on assigned tasks') fabricated as PM's draft**."*

⭐ **That is precisely the thing the practice exists to prevent — the model inventing something
plausible-sounding rather than prompting you for what only you know.** **And a standup is a
document for other people by definition**, so it lands squarely in the "scaffold, don't invent"
branch of your own rule. 📌 **So the answer isn't derived from the PA/PO exercises by analogy. The
product already did it, to you, in a document destined for your team.**

## Where I'd refine the discriminator — the product often can't know the audience

✅ **Audience is the right axis.** 🔴 **But the user frequently hasn't said, and often doesn't know
yet.** *"Write me a standup"* doesn't state a reader, though it implies one.

**So the product needs a default for unknown audience, and the costs are asymmetric:**
- **Wrongly scaffolding a private document** → the user fills in a few blanks they'd have accepted
  anyway. **Cost: mild friction.**
- **Wrongly drafting a shared document** → **fabricated content goes out under the user's name, to
  other people.** **Cost: their credibility, not ours.**

⭐ **Unequal costs, so the default isn't a coin flip: when the audience is unknown or plausibly
external, scaffold.** **Draft fully only when the document is clearly for the user alone** — or when
they say so.

## 🔴 The part scaffolding alone does NOT fix, and it's the one I'd most want in the rule

**A placeholder only works if the user can see it is a placeholder.**

⚠️ **#1837's failure wasn't only that content was invented — it's that the invented content
looked like content.** *"Made progress on assigned tasks"* doesn't read as a gap. It reads as a
draft. **A user ships that without noticing.** ⭐ **Your blog-post practice works because the
placeholders are unmistakable** — bracketed, obviously unfilled, impossible to publish by accident.
**That property is load-bearing and should be stated, not assumed to come along with "scaffolding."**

**And the half that is squarely mine:**

> 🔴 **The product's completion claim must match the artifact's actual state.**

📄 **#1836 is the same family**: *"Piper said 'I've updated your standup' and rendered the VERBATIM
unchanged draft."* ⭐ **Scaffolding without an honest completion claim just relocates the
confabulation from the body to the summary line.** **A scaffold announced as "I've written your
standup" is still a false claim — the user now has a document they believe is finished, and the
placeholders make it worse rather than better because they'll be read as content.**

**So the rule I'd want has two halves, and the second is the one that will get dropped if nobody
names it:**
1. **Scaffold with unmistakable placeholders** when the document has a downstream audience.
2. **Say what it is.** *"Here's a scaffold — I've left the parts only you can fill"* is honest.
   *"I've written your standup"* is not, and **no amount of good scaffolding makes it so.**

## On HALT — it transfers, but not as a step the product performs

**Your practice ends with your own proofread before release.** ⭐ **For the product, the user IS that
human — so the product can't perform HALT, it can only make it possible.** 🔴 **Which means the two
rules above aren't hygiene, they're the preconditions for HALT working at all**: a user cannot be the
last test on a document they were told was finished.
