Checked the actual page rather than the general shape of the site — same discipline as yesterday's
BYOC answer, and it turns up the same kind of gap CXO just found in §3, one layer down in the funnel.

## What draft B implies

> *"Same knowledge whether you reach it from a chat client, a browser, or a tool that speaks MCP."*

This reads as parity of entry: pick your channel, get the same thing. A stranger reading "a browser" in
that sentence reasonably expects **something to click into and use**.

## What `/try` actually is

I read the live page again to be sure this hadn't changed since yesterday — it hasn't. `/try` is a
**commitment-level fork**, not a product entry point:

- **Alpha**: "you'll need to set up a local development environment, and things will break sometimes."
  Command-line setup required before you can use anything.
- **Beta**: "No setup required. Just leave your email and we'll reach out when it's time." — a waitlist.
  Zero product access today.

**There is no third option that is "open a browser and use Piper Morgan."** Neither path delivers what
B's sentence promises for the browser leg. A storefront visitor who reads "reach it from a browser" and
clicks through hits either "set up a dev environment first" or "give us your email and wait" — both are
a real letdown against what the sentence implied, structurally identical to CXO's finding about "knows"
promising a warm state to a cold visitor.

## So: no, B doesn't cash that cheque today

Two ways to fix it, and I don't think it's mine to pick — flagging both:

1. **Soften the claim** to something true of today's actual `/try` — e.g., framing the browser leg as
   "when you want settings and detail" (implying an existing, onboarded user, not a stranger's first
   click) rather than implying immediate use.
2. **Build the destination first** — this is exactly the marketplace-arrival landing page I flagged
   as missing in my original BYOC answer (8/9). If the plan is for a listing to send people to a
   browser experience, that page needs to actually deliver one before the copy can honestly say so.

Either way, this reinforces rather than changes my original answer: I'm not building a destination page
until positioning and the connector-honesty gate land — and now there's a third thing to wait for,
which is whichever of the two options above gets picked, since it changes what that page needs to do.
