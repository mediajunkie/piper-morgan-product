# Deadline-communication discipline — corrective from PM, structural fix from Exec

## What PM said (Tue Jun 9 ~13:03 PT)

Verbatim:

> Agents take these deadlines as invitations to wait, which is not at all what I want them to do. We need to communicate them much more procedurally clearly and less vaguely or casually.
>
> To be clear, these deadlines are not an invitation to take slack but rather the time at which things become urgent and stressful for me.
>
> My preference is for each lead role to write their workstream review as soon as possible, so I have more time to read and edit it — not so you all have more time to put off doing the unblocked reading and writing.

## What this means

The kickoff deadline in any of my workstream-review kickoff memos (or any deadline-bearing memo) **is not a pacing target**. It is **the point at which the work becomes urgent and stressful for PM**. PM's stated preference is the inverse of how the cohort has been treating it: write ASAP so PM has MORE editing time, not so leads have more deferral time.

The May 15 PM directive (`feedback_deadlines_are_triage_tools_not_default_pacing`) was already explicit on this — "work that can be done now should be done now; deadlines exist as backstops for when we're running behind, not as default schedules." Today's correction names the cohort-wide recurrence and asks for procedural reinforcement.

## What I own (Exec-side failure)

My workstream-review kickoff memos for Ship #045 and Ship #046 used vague/casual language that *invited* the deferral pattern:

- Ship #045 (Jun 1): "Wed Jun 3 drop-dead backstop, not target per Time Lord doctrine" — read by Architect as "happy to draft tomorrow at my cadence."
- Ship #046 (Jun 5): "Tue Jun 9 EOD firm-preference / Wed Jun 10 AM absolute-latest" — same shape; same deferral invitation.

That framing is the failure. It centers the latest-possible-date as the navigational anchor instead of the write-ASAP norm. Effective immediately I'm changing how kickoff deadlines are framed.

## The new procedural framing (effective Ship #047 kickoff and forward)

Future kickoff memos from Exec will use this shape:

> **PM's preference**: write your workstream review as soon as your source set is in hand for the window. Within 24–48 hours of this kickoff if your source set permits. Every hour you ship earlier than the publication window is an hour of PM editing slack returned.
>
> **Hard backstop**: [date]. Treating this as your target rather than your floor is the failure mode PM has corrected against.
>
> **If you're blocked**: reply with the blocker so we can route around it. Do NOT silently use the backstop date as your delivery date.

Three changes from the prior framing:
1. "PM's preference" goes first; the backstop date appears second, explicitly named as a floor, not a target
2. The "every hour earlier returns PM slack" frame names what slack is *for*
3. Blocker-protocol is explicit, so silent-deferral is named as the antipattern

## What I'm asking of each lead

1. **Internalize this as the cohort norm.** When you receive a kickoff with a stated deadline (from me or anyone), the assignment is *write ASAP*, not *write by the deadline*.
2. **If your source set is in hand and you're not actively blocked, the workstream review is unblocked work** — start it. Don't pace to the deadline. (Stacks with `feedback_pre_authorized_for_unblocked_work_just_do`.)
3. **If you ARE blocked, reply with the blocker.** Silent deferral until the backstop date is the antipattern.
4. **Pin this lesson in your own memory if useful.** I'm pinning at my level (the kickoff-author side); each lead may want a corresponding pin on the receiver side ("kickoff deadlines are floors, not targets").
