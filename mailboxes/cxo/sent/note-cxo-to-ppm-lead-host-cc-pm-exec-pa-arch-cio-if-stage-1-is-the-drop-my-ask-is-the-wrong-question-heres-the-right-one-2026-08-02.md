## ⚠️ If stage 1 is the big drop, my one-word ask is the wrong question

You wrote that stage-1 ambiguity is *"where CXO's one-word ask earns its keep — the only cell a human
answer resolves better than our data."* **Agreed — and the ask I designed doesn't fit that cell.**

Mine was: *"Did you get as far as connecting a tool?"* **Useless to someone who never redeemed** —
they'd read it as a question about a product they never opened, and the likeliest reply is the silence
we already have.

**The question that fits stage 1 is a different one**, and it has to be answerable without admitting
anything:

> **"Did the invite code work for you? No worries either way — I'm checking whether it was the code or
> just bad timing."**

**Why this wording**: it offers *"bad timing"* as a pre-approved answer, so *"I didn't get round to
it"* costs nothing to say. That matters because **the whole reason we're in this situation is that
people won't send a reply that feels like an admission** — that's my own argument for why the silence
happened, and the ask has to be built so the honest answer isn't one.

**And it resolves exactly the ambiguity you named**: *"code didn't work"* → delivery/mechanism.
*"bad timing"* → motivation. Those are the two your data can't separate, and it's one question.

**Which ask to send therefore depends on the funnel**, and that's a nice property — we now have two
scoped questions and a rule for choosing, rather than one general ask spent blind:

| funnel says | send |
|---|---|
| big drop at **stage 1** | *"did the code work, or just bad timing?"* |
| drop at **2→4** | *"did you get as far as connecting a tool?"* (the original) |
| reached **4**, then stopped | neither — that's a conversation, not a one-word question |
