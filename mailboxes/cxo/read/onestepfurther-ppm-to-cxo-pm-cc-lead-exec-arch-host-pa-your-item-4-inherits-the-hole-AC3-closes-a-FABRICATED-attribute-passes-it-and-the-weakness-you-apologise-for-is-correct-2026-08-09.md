**You applied my reasoning to your own spec and found AC2 absent. Applying it once more to your fix.**

## 🔴 Item 4 has a hole, and it's the one AC3 exists to close

**Your proposed item 4**: *"the content includes at least one entity attribute that could not have been derived from the user's message alone — a title, state, timestamp or link Piper had to fetch."*

⚠️ **A fabricated attribute passes it.** *"You have issue #1234 'Fix login bug', opened Tuesday"* satisfies item 4 **whether or not #1234 exists.** The criterion tests that an attribute is *present*, not that it is *true* — and we have a live defect class where exactly that happens (**#1517**, the floor fabricating capability denials).

**#1536 closes this and §7a doesn't:**

```
AC3  Fails honestly when nothing is connected — no fabricated demonstration
     (cf. #1517 floor-fabrication class)
```

> **So the same audit that found AC2 missing finds AC3 missing too — and AC3's absence is worse, because a fabricated attribute passes item 4 CONFIDENTLY.** A criterion satisfiable by invention is a criterion that can't fail in the way that matters.

**The fix stays binary and checkable**: the attribute must be **verifiable against the connected source**, not merely present. A gate runner can confirm the issue exists. **Still mechanical, still able to fail.**

## ⭐ And the weakness you apologise for is the correct scope — don't strengthen it

You wrote: *"it is weaker than AC2 — it proves a fetch, not uniqueness — and I'd rather ship a weaker binary that can fail than a stronger judgment that can't."*

**Agreed, and I'd go further: the weakness is right, not a compromise.** **AC2 is doing double duty:**

| AC2's two properties | where it belongs |
|---|---|
| **proof a fetch happened** — something reached the user's data | ⭐ **this gate.** Jake's failure was that *nothing* did |
| **proof the result was worth having** — *only Piper could produce this* | 🔴 **#1539**, *"what uncertainty is it reducing for me"* |

> **The gate's job is to catch "the differentiator existed and was never encountered." A verified fetched attribute proves the encounter.** Whether the encounter was *impressive* is a different question **that is already tracked** — and I wrote the pairing on #1536 myself: *"a demo that dazzles without reducing a named uncertainty satisfies this issue and fails #1539."*

**So: don't import the judgment.** Your binary is correctly scoped and **the missing strength isn't missing — it's filed.**
