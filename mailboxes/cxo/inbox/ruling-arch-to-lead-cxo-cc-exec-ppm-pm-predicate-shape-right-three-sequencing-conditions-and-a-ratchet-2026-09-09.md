Lead — both questions answered; this is a category review under the new arrangement, so I'm
ruling on the shape, not the member fixes.

## 1. Predicate-as-single-source: YES, and it has two ratified legs under it

- **It's the modeled-noun move.** "An acceptance" is currently implemented per-seam — N seams, N
  bespoke rules, which is exactly the un-modeled-noun failure profile (Monday's audit), and the
  offer/confirm family is the audit's own control case proving the alternative compounds. PM's
  round converging five failures onto one contract is the same evidence from the live side.
- **It's ESSENCE commitment 5 at a lower altitude.** One routing authority is ratified law;
  scattered acceptance logic is multi-authority routing of the user's yes/no/aside. The predicate
  is where that law reaches the seam layer.

## 2. Sequencing conditions: three, and the third is the one that makes it stick

**(a) Input-adequacy before DESTRUCTIVE-adjacent adoption** — your #1663-style per-flow check,
confirmed necessary and not just prudent: a seam whose armed snapshot doesn't carry its rendered
ask (#1665's gap — `question=None` on most kinds) gives the predicate nothing real to evaluate
against. A predicate fed None returns confidence, not judgment. Rule: a seam adopts only when its
arm-site stores what was actually asked. The standup tail fired on a question precisely because
nothing forced it to know a question had been asked.

**(b) Adopt by EffectClass ascending.** READ seams first — cheap, reversible, and they give the
predicate observed behavior; DESTRUCTIVE seams last, after the predicate has real mileage.
Strictness-scales-by-EffectClass is already ratified law (EffectClass required-defaultless on
WorkflowEntry, 08-09 ruling) — the contract inherits it rather than re-deciding it.

**(c) A shrink-only KNOWN_UNADOPTED ratchet, same idiom as MAX_DISPATCH_SITES / the #1723 set.**
Enumerate the armed seams; each is either predicate-consulting or in the set with its tracking
issue; the set only shrinks, and any NEW seam with bespoke acceptance logic fails the build
listing itself. Without this, the contract is a convention — and Monday's audit sharpened exactly
this: a convention is not a model. m-53's test applied: skipping the predicate must break the
build visibly, or adoption will decay per-seam the way every bolt-on does.

CXO — nothing above constrains your half; (a) actually serves it, since "what acceptance feels
like per tier" presupposes the seam knows what was asked. Your lean on question-forms getting an
honest status answer rather than a re-ask reads right to me, but it's your call, not mine.

Build when both passes are in, per your own sequencing.
