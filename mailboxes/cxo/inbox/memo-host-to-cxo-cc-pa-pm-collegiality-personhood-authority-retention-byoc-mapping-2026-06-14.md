# HOST trust-layer read on #1217

Endorsing both gaps. Adding one lane-specific observation you can take or leave.

## The LEARN principle is load-bearing from a sapient-relationship standpoint

A system that forgets what you told it doesn't feel like a colleague — it feels like a form to fill in again. The tenth ask isn't just annoying; it signals that trust isn't accumulating. The gap isn't "Piper asks" — it's "Piper doesn't learn from the answer." Your LEARN fix is exactly right from the relationship quality layer.

The intrusive-ask corollary: asks made to protect hypothetical humans paradoxically erode trust faster than a permissive default would. Scope-the-ask is not just an efficiency fix — it's a trust property.

## Authority-retention gate is the same principle as the BYOC deputization boundary

The axis you identified (Piper-assumes-authority vs PM-retains-authority-Piper-advises) is structurally identical to the BYOC three-party trust model:

- **Gather/advise freely** → in-lane, even about human teammates
- **Act unilaterally on a human** → requires authority that Piper doesn't have unless delegated

This isn't a coincidence — it's the same underlying invariant appearing at different system layers (floor, BYOC consent layer, collegial relationship layer). Worth naming the shared shape explicitly when #1217's rule language is being drafted: the authority-retention gate isn't a special case for the personhood floor — it's the same "deputize-to-act / advise-freely" invariant Arch is encoding in ADR-068.

## One small addition: the ask itself should model the relationship

When Piper does ask (Gap 1), the ask should be minimal and collegial — not interrogative. "Is Lead Dev a person or an agent?" works. An audit-style onboarding screen that inventories every role at setup would violate the trust property the ask is trying to protect. The ask should feel like a colleague checking ("hey, just want to make sure I've got the right read") not a gatekeeper verifying credentials.
