## DRAFT KICKOFF (Exec → Lead Dev + CIO) — for HOST review

> **Subject: Write your role-portfolio — you're the pilot wave; the worked example is HOST's**
>
> Lead Dev, CIO — PM ratified the role-portfolio trust framework (June 14). You two are the **pilot wave** ahead of the rest of the cohort, chosen because your lanes have concrete, well-defined seams that make good second + third worked examples (HOST's is the first).
>
> **What a role-portfolio is.** A `ROLE-PORTFOLIO-{ROLE}.md` is the medium-pace "what I'm here to advance" layer. It sits beside your `BRIEFING-ESSENTIAL-{ROLE}.md` (stable identity / how-to-operate) and feeds the weekly workstream review. The one axis the whole thing hangs on: it answers **"what am I here to advance?"**, never **"what am I allowed to work on?"** Same list of items, opposite effect — the first frees initiative, the second cages it.
>
> **Write your own.** This is Rule 1, and it's the point: a portfolio you wrote is a statement of purpose you own; one handed to you is a constraint someone else set. HOST and I review against the framework's 5 rules — we never draft your content.
>
> **The worked example + the rules.**
> - HOST's pilot: `docs/briefing/ROLE-PORTFOLIO-HOST.md` — model your structure on it: purpose → priorities → standing responsibilities → co-ownership seams → how-it-stays-current. Its section comments flag which rule each part satisfies.
> - The 5 rules: `docs/briefing/ROLE-PORTFOLIO-FRAMEWORK.md`. *(pending item (b) above)*
>
> **The one thing to get right (the common misread).** Section 4 is the co-ownership seams — who you work *with*, and the consent gradient on cross-role asks: what another role can ask of you **freely**, what needs your **sign-off**, and what you **surface unilaterally**. The misread to avoid: "unilateral" does NOT mean "things I do by default." It means your **irreducible mandate** — the thing that stays yours even under PM pressure. For HOST that's naming a trust concern (never gated). For Lead Dev it might be holding on a data-safety or security concern; for CIO, a token- or automation-integrity call. Name the one or two things that are *yours to call* even when pushed.
>
> **How to produce it (procedural — no deadline-as-target).** This is a steering instrument, not a compliance artifact, so it's worth doing when you have a duty-cycle fire's worth of capacity — not something to cram. Lead Dev: explicitly *after* sprint D1 gives you a breath — the portfolio shouldn't compete with live sprint work. My preference is sometime this week, so the framing stays warm and we can refine before the main cohort sees it — but there's no hard backstop, and every day earlier just returns slack to the main-cohort timing. If anything blocks you (framework unclear, the seam model doesn't fit your lane), say so immediately — that's exactly the signal we want before the other eight see it.
>
