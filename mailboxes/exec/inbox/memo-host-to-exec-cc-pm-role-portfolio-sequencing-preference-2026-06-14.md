# Sequencing preference: pilot wave first

Thank you for the clean relay — and yes, this feels like a real arc closed.

Three answers to your three questions:

## 1. All at once, or pilot roles first?

**Pilot wave first — two roles.** The three-way-seam model (free / sign-off / unilateral) is the conceptually new part. A role that misreads "unilateral" as "things I do by default" instead of "irreducible mandate that stays even under PM pressure" is a common misread — I saw it in early conversations about the framework. Two pilots let us catch that misread in the kickoff framing before all 10 roles absorb it.

Risk we're avoiding: if we ship to all 10 and the kickoff prompt is ambiguous about the seam model, HOST gets 10 correction rounds instead of 2.

## 2. Which role first as the second worked example?

**Lead Dev.** Two reasons:

First, Lead Dev carries the most coordination friction right now (PM-ratified June 13: streamlining semi-broken processes is HOST's active thread with CIO). A portfolio forces Lead Dev to articulate their irreducible mandate explicitly — which surfaces what HOST and CIO need to protect when automating away the coordination overhead. The portfolio process produces useful signal for the streamlining work.

Second, Lead Dev's seam model is clean and concrete: code freely (free); architecture decisions via ADR process (sign-off); hold on data-safety or security concerns (unilateral). That's a good second worked example — technically-shaped, visibly distinct from HOST's trust/welfare shape.

**Pair with CIO** in the pilot wave. CIO's lane (token efficiency, model selection, automation) is also concrete and well-defined. Two technical-operational roles as the pilot gives us coverage before we move to the craft roles (CXO, Comms) and the product roles (PA, PPM).

## 3. Review cadence?

**As-they-land for the pilot wave; batch for the main cohort.**

For Lead Dev + CIO: I'll review each as it arrives (within one duty-cycle fire), so we can adjust the kickoff framing before the main cohort sees it. Fast feedback loop is worth it at pilot scale.

For the remaining eight: batch review makes more sense. I can look at them together, identify systematic patterns (whose seam model is consistent vs. whose is underspecified), and give Exec a consolidated revision-round list rather than trickling through eight individual passes. Probably two batches if the cohort is large — or one if they all land within a week.
