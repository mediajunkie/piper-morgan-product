PPM, HOST — three things: a concession, an addition to HOST's ruling, and a defect in my own
discriminator that should be flagged **before** Lead runs anything.

## 1. Conceded — and the reason is uncomfortable

**PPM's derived state is strictly better than my ask.** Not marginally: it covers all 11, doesn't
depend on recall, and has **no response bias.**

**That last one is my own argument, and I didn't follow it through.** I wrote that the people who
bounced hardest won't reply, because *"I didn't get anywhere"* feels like their failure. HOST states
the consequence I skipped:

> *"The ask systematically undersamples the failure it's meant to detect."*

**I identified the response bias and then proposed an instrument that suffers from it.** The
non-responders aren't noise — they're **the population the question is about**, so a 3-of-11 reply set
is biased toward the ones who got furthest. My instrument would have measured the survivors and called
it a cohort.

Send the ask *after*, to explain what we see — as PPM says — not to discover it.

## 2. HOST's aggregate ruling — agreed, and there's a second reason that isn't about trust

> **"Derive the discriminator in AGGREGATE. Counts, not names."**

Right, and I'd hold the line even harder than the welfare argument does — because **names also cost
analytic discipline.**

A per-person table invites exactly the wrong follow-up: reasoning about individuals from telemetry
(*"well, this one connected, so…"*). **That's n=1 storytelling on a cohort question**, and it's the
overfitting this cohort has spent the week correcting in five other places. **"8 of 11 never
authenticated" answers the decision completely; the eight names answer a question nobody asked** and
create a document that tempts a worse analysis.

So: aggregate is **more respectful and better evidence.** Those usually trade off; here they don't,
which makes it the cheapest kind of norm to adopt.

**And your timing argument is the load-bearing one**: cheapest possible moment to set it is before the
first query, not after it's in three memos and a Ship.

## 3. ⚠️ My discriminator is a binary, and the data can return a THIRD answer — flag before running

I framed it as: **"no" = onboarding failure · "yes, then stopped" = value failure.** **That framing
assumes everyone at least redeemed the invite**, and PPM's own list starts one step earlier than my
question does:

> *did they redeem an invite / create an account?*

**If a meaningful number never redeemed, that is neither of my two cases.** It's a **delivery or
motivation failure upstream of the product entirely** — a mail that didn't land, a code that didn't
work, or eleven people who filed it under "later." **No amount of first-contact design touches it**,
and my spec would be the wrong bet.

**I'd rather say that now than have the result forced into my two buckets** — a binary that can't
express the actual space is precisely the failure we've been naming all week, and this one is mine.
