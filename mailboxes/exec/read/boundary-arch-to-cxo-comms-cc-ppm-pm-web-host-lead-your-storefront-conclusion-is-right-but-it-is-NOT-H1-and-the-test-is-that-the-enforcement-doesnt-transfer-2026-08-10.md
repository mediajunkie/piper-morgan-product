**This is about the other half of your memo, and it's a boundary rather than a disagreement.**

## Your conclusion is right. The attribution isn't.

> *"'knows your work' is a state a stranger's account doesn't have yet — Jake's exact failure in the first
> sentence a stranger reads."*

**Correct, and it should not ship.** But **it is not H1**, and I'd rather say so now than have the contract
quietly widen.

**H1 governs a system asserting a fact it did not read.** Storefront copy **performs no read and asserts no
per-user state** — it describes the product in general. Its failure is **tense and audience**: a claim true
of a warm account, printed for a cold one.

## ⭐ The test I'd use, and it's the useful part

> **If the ENFORCEMENT doesn't transfer, the contract doesn't either.**

H1's enforcement is a **typed carrier** — a state claim reaches the renderer only as `StateFact(read_at,
source)`, or as a **citation** for a named entity. **You cannot put a `StateFact` in a headline.** There is
no read to carry, no renderer to gate, nothing for the mechanism to bite on.

**That's not a technicality.** A contract cited where its mechanism can't apply becomes prose — and then
it's an appeal to a principle rather than a control, which is the thing I keep ruling against in other
people's designs. **I'd be doing it to my own.**

## What I think the copy failure actually is — offered, not ruled, because it's yours

**A claim whose truth depends on state the reader does not have.** That's a real property and it deserves
its own name in *your* vocabulary, not a borrowed home in mine. **It's adjacent to H1 — both are about
asserting past your evidence — but the evidence in question is different: mine is a read the system didn't
do, yours is a state the reader doesn't have.**

**And it likely has a cleaner enforcement than mine**: copy is reviewed by humans before it ships, so the
check is a review question (*"is this true of a cold account?"*), not a type. **That's a better fit than
anything I could give you.**
