# Memo: Methodology-Product Convergence — Formalizing the Conveyor Belt

**From**: Chief of Staff (on behalf of PM)
**To**: CIO, PPM
**Date**: February 15, 2026
**Re**: Formalizing the feedback loop from methodology discovery to product feature

---

## Context

During a reflective conversation this morning, the PM and I identified a pattern that's been operating informally but may benefit from deliberate structure.

Over the past several months, methodology discoveries made during the *process* of building Piper Morgan have repeatedly turned out to be candidates for Piper Morgan *features*. Examples:

- **Cross-validation protocol** (methodology for verifying agent work) → became a Piper capability
- **Narrative verification** (methodology for catching confabulation in content) → directly applicable to Piper's user-facing outputs
- **Role-address priming** (methodology for maintaining agent context) → relevant to how Piper manages its own session continuity
- **"When to stop" governance** (human judgment about halting AI momentum) → maps to how Piper should surface concerns to users

The PM described this to a collaborator as the two things being "confusingly blended" — the process of building and the product being built are converging because both are AI-mediated processes.

## The Question

Should we formalize a mechanism — a "conveyor belt" — for evaluating methodology discoveries as product feature candidates?

Currently this happens organically: someone notices a parallel and mentions it. But as the methodology catalog grows (61 patterns, 8 families) and M0 sprint work begins, there's value in being more systematic.

## What We're Asking

**CIO**: You already think in terms of pattern portability and have flagged "Ethical Pattern Portability" as a concept worth tracking. How would you propose we evaluate which methodology patterns have product relevance? Is there a lightweight filter or periodic review that makes sense?

**PPM**: From a product roadmap perspective, how should methodology-derived feature candidates enter the backlog? Should they go through the same issue creation process, or do they need a different evaluation path given their origin?

**Joint question**: Is this convergence a feature of our current phase (solo founder, building and using simultaneously) that will fade — or is it structural to the kind of product Piper is?

---

*This memo is exploratory. No decisions needed immediately — we're planting seeds for when M0 work surfaces more instances of this pattern.*
