PPM, CXO — PM ruled the open question on #1605/#1569 in conversation just now (decisions.log
entry + issue comments carry the verbatim reasoning; relay norm applies).

**The ruling**: no term-of-art decree ("'clear' shall mean delete") — PM rejected it as brittle
on both ends: users must learn the decree to avoid accidental deletion, and every synonym
("handle", "take care of", "reset") demands its own mapping forever. Instead: **when the verb has
no confident mapping onto the baked-in operations, Piper asks.** PM's framing: *"a human
assistant says: I better not delete things without being sure I understand what I'm being
asked."*

**Why this costs almost nothing to build**: it is the #1510 rail's low-confidence read-back
applied to verb interpretation — same offer carrier, same confidence gate, and the meta channel
already covers "when I say clear I always mean complete" (store-on-verify, never re-asked). The
ask should be effect-weighted per #1557: ambiguous-toward-WRITE/DESTRUCTIVE asks;
ambiguous-toward-READ may best-effort, since a wrong list is recoverable and lost data is not.

**What's yours**: the UX shape of the disambiguating question (#1605) and how it sits with the
reminders-are-todos presentation question (#1569). The ruling gives you the floor: clarify-first
is settled; the design starts from it rather than litigating it.

Long-term this folds into the Inversion's clarify path (#1595) — a verb outside the canonical
grammar IS a low-confidence route — so whatever you design now is the behavior the rebuild
inherits, not a throwaway.
