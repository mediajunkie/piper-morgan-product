## Key Concepts from TUG

### 1. Warranted Trust (Vocabulary Upgrade)

We've been calling it "trust gradient." TUG's framing is better:
- Not "we want the technology to be trusted"
- But "we want people to have a *warranted* level of trust"
- Includes the idea that too *little* trust is also a problem

**Talk angle**: "The goal isn't building trust — it's building *warranted* trust. Piper earns proactivity privileges incrementally, and can lose them. The trust should match reality."

### 2. Three Validity Claims (Habermas)

| Claim | Definition | Can AI Do This? |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| **Factual** | Is it true? | ✅ Yes |
| **Ethical** | Aligned with values? | ✅ Yes |
| **Authentic** | From genuine experience? | ❌ No |

**Talk angle**: "Piper can be accurate and ethical, but it can't be *authentic* — it doesn't have experiences. Our Professional Boundaries principle acknowledges this. When Piper explains its limitations, it's being authentic *about* its lack of authenticity."

### 3. Ethics as Infrastructure (Shared Principle)

Both TUG and Piper Morgan independently arrived at: you can't paste ethics on as an afterthought. It has to be architectural.

**Talk angle**: "Just like you can't create a user experience at the last minute — you're creating it the whole time — you can't add ethics at the end. The ethical behavior of the system gets baked in at the core."

### 4. Social Interface Design → AI Interface Design

xian's earlier work on social interface design (Yahoo, O'Reilly book) turns out to be directly relevant. When you create social environments, you're responsible for what happens in them. AI agents are now participating in social interfaces using language.

**Talk angle**: "Everything I learned about social experience design in the 2000s applies here. The ethical implications of built information spaces are heavy. Now we have non-human but sapient actors participating in those spaces."
