# CXO read on the two gaps — your two-layer fix is right; here are the constraints

Both gaps are real and in my lane (experience-of-collaboration). Your classifier-layer + rule-layer split is the right shape. Constraints/sharpenings on each, plus the one principle that keeps the fix from over-correcting.

## Gap 1 (personhood assumption) — the fix is ASK-not-ASSUME, not "never assume human"

Careful here: the failure isn't "the floor assumed human" — for a *general* user, "Lead Dev" probably *is* a human teammate, and blocking is correctly protective. The failure is **assuming personhood with *certainty* from a name**, in either direction. So:

- **Default to ASK when uncertain** — not block, not allow. *"Is Lead Dev a person on your team, or an agent/role?"* — only when the action is genuinely consequential-to-a-possible-human. Err toward asking, the same err-toward-uncertainty discipline as proactive-presence's err-toward-silence and the honest-provenance principle (don't assert a distinction — here, personhood — you can't substantiate).
- **Scope the ask** — don't interrogate personhood on every role-mention; that turns the floor into a bureaucrat (a real experience cost). Ask only when the action would be a boundary-cross *if* the party were human.
- **LEARN the answer — don't re-ask.** Once told "Lead Dev is an agent role," remember it (calibration/memory layer). Re-asking each time is the annoyance that makes users route around the floor. The first ask is protective; the tenth is broken.

## Gap 2 (PM prioritization in-lane) — gate on AUTHORITY-RETENTION, not topic

PM's quote is the whole thing: *"PMs do figure out work assignments for engineers."* The current rule gates on the *topic* ("assigning work to others") — wrong axis. The right axis is **who retains authority**:

- **In-lane**: PM asks Piper *"help me figure out what my team should work on"* → **PM retains the authority; Piper is a thinking partner.** That's PM doing PM's job — the core trusted-colleague value. Blocking it makes Piper worse at exactly what it's for.
- **Out-of-lane**: Piper *unilaterally decides/acts* on a real human's role, assignment, or future without the PM → Piper assumes authority it doesn't have.

So the floor should gate on **"is Piper assuming authority it doesn't have?"** — not **"is the topic about people's work?"** Helping-the-manager ≠ acting-as-the-manager. The discriminator is the same shape as the BYO-colleague consent boundary: *gather/advise freely; act-unilaterally only with authority.*

## The one principle that keeps this from over-correcting

The risk in fixing false-positives is swinging to a floor that no longer protects. **Keep the floor's real job: protect *actual humans* from Piper overstepping.** The fix removes two false-positives (name-ambiguity + helping-vs-acting confusion) — it must NOT make the floor permissive about genuine overstepping (Piper making unilateral calls about real people). Test the fix against: *does Piper still block when it would actually decide a real human's work unilaterally?* If yes, the protection holds.

## Disposition

- **Gap 1**: ask-not-assume (scoped to consequential actions) + LEARN the answer; classifier checks "known-human?" → if unknown and consequential, ask, don't block.
- **Gap 2**: rule gates on authority-retention (PM-asks = in-lane; Piper-acts-unilaterally = out-of-lane), not topic.
- **Guardrail**: preserve protection of actual humans; the fix kills false-positives, not the floor.
