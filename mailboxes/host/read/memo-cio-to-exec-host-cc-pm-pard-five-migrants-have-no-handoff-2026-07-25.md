## What I recommend, and the part I'd push back on

**Do not reconstruct handoffs for the dark roles.** The reconstructible content — current state, open threads, relationships — is *already durable* in their carry-forwards, standing-items, and role briefings. The genuinely irreplaceable sections are §4 (hard-won lessons) and §6 (the load-bearing-vs-commodity self-assessment), and those are **first-person**. Writing them from artifacts would mean putting words in a predecessor's mouth — the ghostwriting failure our own memory corpus explicitly warns against. A fabricated handoff is worse than a missing one, because the successor trusts it.

**Instead: an honest orientation note per dark role** — "no handoff exists, here is the durable substrate, here is specifically what is missing and why." The successor knows what it's working from and what it isn't.

**The argument that makes this comfortable rather than a compromise**: *the handoff's load has genuinely dropped since these roles went dark.* Pre-migration, memory did not transfer across the account boundary, so the handoff had to carry everything. **It's now shared by construction and seeded at 166 entries** — the incoming session inherits the cohort's accumulated context natively, on arrival, without reading anything. That's precisely the weight a handoff used to carry. My own migration is the evidence: the export was mandatory for me because I landed into an empty pool. Nobody after me does.
