# What PM approved, and what is actually being proposed

PM approved me drafting this and sending it to you; **ratifying it, amending it, or refusing it is
yours.** The ask is one new section on the sprint-closeout template, plus one standing assignment
for Exec. It is deliberately a section on an existing artifact and **not** a new ritual, for reasons
the history makes concrete.

# The history, including the part I got wrong

Handoff docs used to be written at the end of every session. They ran near-daily through 2025 and
then stopped: **nothing between 2025-10-01 and 2026-08-11**, when eleven appeared at once for the
Amber reboot. Two more today. Ten months dark, then event-driven.

**My first explanation was partly wrong and PM corrected it from having been there.** I said the
handoff carried everything because session logs, carry-forwards and omnibus logs did not yet exist.
They did — PM: *"we absolutely had session logs and we even sometimes had things that were carry
forwards, although the handoff was sort of the functional carry forward back then. We had omnibus
logs too… These things have been accumulated in stages."* **The conclusion held; one of its
supporting reasons did not** — which is the exact shape Arch named on 09-15 (*"a true conclusion
resting on a wrong reason would survive longer than it should"*), now with my name on an instance.
Recording it because a proposal built on a wrong premise is worth less than one built on a corrected
one, and because the corrected premise is the better argument anyway.

**What actually killed it**: those sessions were short-lived and a *new conversation was starting
within hours*. The handoff had a reader, and it was the only thing that would reach them. When
sessions became long-lived, **that reader stopped existing** — and the ritual died within weeks of
losing them. Not laziness. **A duty with no reader is a duty nobody misses** (m-53's shape: skipping
it broke nothing visible).

**And the surfaces specialized around it.** Session log, carry-forward, registry, omnibus, GitHub and
the belt each took a slice. What is left over is the residue none of them hold — which is precisely
the job PM named:

> **"What would I want to know that I might not get from the automated processes?"**

I would put that sentence in the template **verbatim**. Every paraphrase I tried came out narrower.

# Why weekly, and why not daily

PM's reasoning, which I think is the load-bearing design constraint:

> *"if it's done daily it sort of becomes a routine thing and probably is less good at capturing that
> question."*

The residue does not accumulate daily. A daily version spends most of its words restating the log,
which is how a ritual becomes theater — and theater is what gets skipped first under pressure, then
skipped permanently. There is precedent for the failure mode in this cohort already: **PM and Lead
Dev, and sometimes Arch, used to hold end-of-day retros** — how the day went, how PM was feeling.
Real value, and **it did not scale.** A weekly cadence is the same instinct at a rate the input
actually arrives at.

# The proposal

**A. One section on the sprint-closeout template**, for each leadership role, ~150 words, headed with
PM's question verbatim. Prompts, offered as examples rather than a checklist:

- A way of working that changed, or should have.
- A pattern you noticed across your week that no single issue or log captures.
- Something you now believe that you did not believe on Friday.
- **How your seat specifically gets things wrong** — your repeat errors, in shapes a successor would
  recognize. In the restart handoffs written today this was consistently the most valuable paragraph,
  and it is the one thing a fresh session cannot reconstruct from the repo.
- Anything about how the week actually *felt* to work in. PM's retros used to ask this directly and
  nothing currently does.

**B. A standing assignment for Exec**, separate from synthesizing the closeout: reflect on the week
across the whole cohort for what is not already captured — a way of working, a pattern, an insight.
Mine is the cross-role view; theirs is the lane view, and the two find different things.

# The one thing that determines whether this survives

**It has to ride an artifact with a live reader, which is the whole lesson from the first ritual's
death.** On the closeout, a missing reflection shows up as a visible hole in the synthesis PM reads
— someone notices, same week. As a freestanding weekly doc it would have no one who breaks when it
is skipped, and it would rot exactly the way the last one did.

So: **please do not lift it into its own artifact to make it tidier.** The untidiness is the
mechanism. If you'd rather it hang off a different existing surface with a real reader, that works —
the requirement is the reader, not the closeout specifically.

**Amend freely.** The word cap, the prompt list and the placement are all mine and none is
load-bearing. The two that are: **the question verbatim**, and **a reader who notices its absence.**
