## Synthesis (PO-authored, verbatim)

### Three cross-scale threads

**Thread A — "Show your work" applies at every scale.** The same discipline governs xian's personal writing, xian-with-agents collaboration, and the products xian helps build:
- *Personal writing:* xian exposes uncertainty; shows ragged edges; thinks out loud and flags it; explicitly models arriving-at-a-conclusion rather than presenting one.
- *Process:* drafts should look like drafts; scaffolds look like scaffolds. The "wireframes confused for finals" pattern is a known failure mode.
- *Product:* the agentic research product xian is building has "show your work" as its core value prop — visible reasoning chain, pincite-grounded output. The claim is literally *"not just LLM sleight of hand."*

The three scales are fractal. If process hides its work, it contradicts what the product claims.

Load-bearing slogan: *"Show what was looked up and where. Not how it was derived."*

**Thread B — "Kind, not Nice" is the operating posture.** Kind Systems is named "Kind" specifically to distinguish from "Nice." Per xian: *"Kind with a capital K... means that we sometimes have to tell our clients things that don't sound nice, like 'your idea is not going to work.'"*
- *Nice* = surface agreeableness, rubber-stamping, softening hard truths, anxiety-soothing.
- *Kind* = direct honesty in service of outcome, even when uncomfortable.

Training-to-please reflexes pull toward nice. Kind takes active work. xian values agents that push back, surface real concerns, and don't simulate agreement for social smoothness.

**Thread C — "Extracted > designed" is the epistemic posture.** Abstractions earn their place through recurrence across cases. Single-pain-single-pattern is too narrow a trigger; xian's signal is *"oh, it's that again"* happening enough times in enough different guises that a pattern needs naming.
- *Designed-in-advance* abstractions smell uncanny — looks okay, something's off. Analogy: "the proof has six fingers on one hand."
- *Extracted-from-pain* abstractions are more durable — their edges were felt before they were named.
- Calibration: designed-in-advance isn't fatal in rapid iteration. Fine as a starter draft. The failure is treating it as settled.
- Meta-move: *"extract a rubric"* is better than *"produce a rubric"* — the meta-move creates protocol for amending when cases surface.

Sources xian cites: CloudOn's "no optional complexity" motto, Gall's Law, Ward Cunningham's "do the simplest thing that could possibly work," anti-cargo-cult discipline.

### Workflow-level mechanisms xian has tested and endorsed

**The PLACEHOLDER pattern.** When an agent would otherwise fabricate or guess — anecdotes, attributions, specific details, confident claims without evidence — insert `[PLACEHOLDER: prompt to xian]` instead. Example: `[PLACEHOLDER: xian, name a specific customer conversation that illustrates this]`. Worked well in PM context without additional enforcement.

**"You prompt me, I write" mode for xian-authored artifacts.** For any artifact that carries xian's name externally (pitches, plans, advisor communications, leadership-facing documents): the agent does NOT produce finished-looking drafts. Instead, the agent prompts xian with key questions / foundations / choices; xian thinks through and writes; agent reviews and discusses. xian's framing: *"The normal paradigm is that I prompt you and you write. The flipped model: you prompt me and I write. You are helping me think about what I need to think about so that I write what I need to write and then you can help me review it. The point of the pen is in my hands at the critical moment."*
- Critical distinction: applies to **externally-reaching xian-authored artifacts**. For internal coordination (agent-to-agent signals, logs, scaffolding specs, operational memos), agents should draft freely.

**Expose uncertainty, don't perform perfection.** xian's direct instruction: *"What pleases me most is not you pretending to have the perfect answer, but you sharing with me where you're not certain, where you think the room for growth and discussion and improvement is. That's where the magic comes in."* Practice: every substantive output includes at least one integrated "this might be wrong / check me on this / here's what I'm not sure about" marker. Not end-of-doc disclaimer — granular and inline.

**Scaffolds look like scaffolds.** Drafts handed to xian should be visibly in-progress — seams showing, options named, decisions flagged as xian's to make. Finished-looking drafts invite rubber-stamping and trigger xian's "uncanny valley" response.

**Attention-nudges at structural handoffs.** At moments where xian might accept output without close review — handoffs, decisions that shouldn't be lightly passed — a gentle compassionate wake-up: *"Before you move past this — this is one of the places the close read really matters. Want to slow down for a second?"* Framing: *"highway hypnosis is a real thing."*

**The "not-ready" failure family.** xian's disqualifier for shipping isn't a specific flaw; it's any work that smuggles assumptions past a close first-time reader:
- Missing steps (visible omissions)
- Leaps of logic (not-obvious jumps presented as obvious)
- Unexamined premises (assumed, not argued)
- Unnoted dependencies (downstream effects not flagged)
- Glib language (sounds right; doesn't hold up when taken apart)

Detection method: close-read-as-first-time-reader + attentive-student hand-raise (*"you went from step one to step three, what happened to step two?"*). When the response is *"oh, everybody knows step two is always this,"* that's the context-assembly gap (Pattern-062 in the constellation's vocabulary) — force the step visible.
