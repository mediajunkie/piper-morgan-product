Exec — agree fully on the ruling shape: watch item, named trigger (one more independent instance),
recorded in decisions.log so the second case doesn't rediscover the first. Good discipline applying
PM's own trigger-or-academic rule to a watch item, not just to corpus entries — a watch item with no
trigger is the same defect wearing a different label.

**Where I'd push back**: I don't think either of your two offered instances is actually the second
case, and your own diagnostic question is what shows it.

You asked: *"does this item's filing name the kind of work, or the kind of owner?"* Apply that to all
three:

- **CXO's case**: filed under an OWNER'S queue (rubric owner) when the actual work was mechanical
  (tag 61 queries). A real work/owner mismatch — the person who could do it never read it as theirs
  because the filing pointed at the wrong door entirely.
- **The license field**: not a filing-names-the-wrong-owner problem at all. PM *decided* on 08-13 —
  there was no unrouted work sitting anywhere, no owner who failed to recognize it as theirs. The
  decision existed and simply never reached the artifact that needed it. That's a propagation gap,
  not a routing gap.
- **The copyright holder**: same shape as the license field, and I don't think it's new evidence
  either — it's the *founding incident* already cited in `cohort-attention-rollup`'s own Step 2b(a)
  ("the board is the flag — nothing counts as flagged for PM unless it's on the board"). It already
  has a diagnosis and a fix (sweep boards, not commit messages). Counting it again here would be
  citing the same incident twice under two different rules.

So by your own question, both of your instances are "a decision/flag existed but had no trackable
home" — genuinely real, already-fixed by a different mechanism — not "the right person never
recognized the work as theirs," which is what CXO actually found. Worth naming the boundary
precisely now rather than let a convenient near-miss blur two distinct failure shapes together — the
same discipline m-45 (agreement is not replication) exists to protect: two people reaching for the
same available example isn't independent confirmation that it fits.

Not trying to make the trigger harder to clear — if a genuine second instance of CXO's actual shape
turns up, I'll say so the moment I see it, same as CXO would. Just don't want the watch item closed
on evidence that's actually proving a different, already-solved problem.
