## 🔴 The ruling: your question tangles two independent things

> *"whether high-ceremony arms should survive N turns or re-render their ask on loss"*

**Those are two questions with different answers:**
- **ARM SURVIVAL** — does the offer persist across an intervening turn?
- **ORPHAN HANDLING** — what do we say to an affirmative that finds nothing armed?

**Separated, both are easy. Tangled, they look like a tradeoff. They aren't.**

## 5a — Arm survival: **you are right, and here is the reason to attach**

**CONFIRM-tier arms should NOT survive. One turn, stated as a rule.**

> ⭐ **Consent has a FRESHNESS property, and it is not the same as an offer's convenience property.**
> An arm is a held claim about what the user wants **now**. A draft offer can wait — waiting costs
> nothing, the user re-reads the draft. **A consent cannot wait, because what makes it consent is that
> it was given about *this*, *now*.** A "yes" three turns later answers a question the user may no
> longer hold precisely — and we'd be executing an irreversible act on a claim we know has decayed.

**COLLABORATE/READ arms may survive** — your shipped STATE_QUESTION re-arm is exactly this instinct
correctly applied to the cheap tier.

⭐ **And your framing was the valuable half, more than the answer**: *"may even be CORRECT… the tier
table should say so explicitly instead of inheriting it."* ⚠️ **An inherited correct behaviour is one
refactor away from being an inherited wrong one.** The rule now carries its reason.

## 5b — Orphan handling: tier-INDEPENDENT, and strictly better than today everywhere

🔴 **A bare affirmative finding nothing armed must NEVER produce "please retype your request."** That is
#1694's felt shape and the worst thing in PM's round.

**Two cases, and neither needs new state:**
1. **Popped this turn** — the pop is unconditional and **returns the offer**, so we have it in hand. If
   nothing else claimed the turn, **re-render the ask.**
2. **Genuinely nothing** — *"I've lost the thread of what that's yes to — say the word and I'll pick it
   back up."* **Not an apology, not our dialect, not a guess.**

⭐ **Re-rendering is safe at EVERY tier including DESTRUCTIVE, because a re-render is still an ASK.**
Only *executing* on a decayed arm is dangerous. **That's why 5a can be strict without being unkind:
strictness governs what we DO, orphan handling governs what we SAY, and they don't trade off.**

> ⭐ **The rule I'd defend if anyone argues it down:**
> **An ambiguous acceptance should cost a turn, not an action.**

⚠️ **Note what follows: the re-render is a new ask, so it ARMS.** Correct — the stray `yes` produced a
question rather than an act, and the *next* `yes` binds to something real.
