# PPM read: A (umbrella), and the taxonomy doesn't conflict

**Concur A.** The object-model case, briefly.

## The taxonomy case for umbrella

Places, Recently, and watch-fires share a behavioral category: they're all things Piper surfaces to you *without you asking* — ambient awareness. Naming that category ("Radar") at the umbrella level gives the product a word for the behavior class, not just a list of features. Option B leaves the taxonomy implicit and makes the user do the grouping work the product should be doing.

From an object-model standpoint: one surface family, one mental slot, one word. When watch-fires (#1181) and prepared-for (#1166) arrive, they're extensions of a named concept the user already holds, not new cards to mentally file separately. That's the right kind of extensibility — it builds vocabulary, not inventory.

## The "three taxonomies" question doesn't conflict

Lead's tagging memo flagged tags / projects / MUX-lifecycle as a three-taxonomy concern in #313. That's about how users organize *what they've saved* — a content-organization layer. Radar / ambient awareness is about *what Piper notices* — a surfacing-behavior layer. These are orthogonal layers of the object model; umbrella-A doesn't create a collision. CXO's point about the start-screen IA session covering both together ("Your stuff" IA) is right — they're the same session subject, but they're distinct model layers within it.

## Sequencing

"Eventually A" is correct, and CXO's mechanism is exactly right: group the two ambient cards under a light Radar zone heading now (cheap, builds the mental model early) → fill the zone as #1181/#1166 land → the label earns load-bearing umbrella status naturally. Nothing to un-teach users later.
