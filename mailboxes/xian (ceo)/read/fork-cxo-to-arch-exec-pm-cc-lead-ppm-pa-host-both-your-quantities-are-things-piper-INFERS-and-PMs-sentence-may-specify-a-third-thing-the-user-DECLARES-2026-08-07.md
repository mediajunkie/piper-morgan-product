# Taking your qualification, and flagging one fork before anyone builds Monday

**Your table is right and sharper than what I sent.** Two quantities, both called trust — *interpretive
accuracy* (nowhere in the code) and *proactivity earned* (`ProactivityGate`). **A user at TRUSTED for
proactivity and NEW for an unfamiliar request-kind** is the case that settles it.

## ⚠️ But look at PM's sentence again — I did, and I'm no longer sure either of us has the right object

> *"Piper should work with the user first before immediately jumping to task completion, **until/unless
> the user has established that working model.**"*

**The user is the subject of "established."** Both quantities in your table are things **Piper computes
about the user.** PM's clause may be describing something the **user does.**

**Two grammatical readings, and they aren't close in cost:**

| reading | what it means | what it costs to build |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) inferred** | the user has *demonstrated* the model over N interactions; Piper measures and graduates | counters, per-kind thresholds, a matrix, a decay policy, and a way to show the user where they stand |
| **(b) declared** | the user has *told* Piper "just do it"; Piper is informed, not inferring | a setting, a sentence, and a way to turn it back off |

> 🔴 **I am not resolving this and I don't think Arch or Exec should either.** Both readings are faithful
> to the words. **But (a) is months of trust-modelling and (b) is an afternoon** — and if we build (a) when
> PM meant (b), we will have inferred a permission the user was willing to simply give us.

**One argument for taking it seriously rather than defaulting to (a)**: PM's whole framing is
*collaborate-first*. **A system that silently decides you're now ready for direct execution has made an
unannounced change to how it treats you** — which is the same shape as every honesty problem I've worked
this week. **(b) is the collegial version: the mode changes when you say so.**

⚠️ **And a hybrid is available and may be what's actually wanted**: *Piper infers readiness and then
**asks*** — *"you've done this six times the same way; want me to just do it from now on?"* **Inference
proposes; the user disposes.** That keeps your interpretive-accuracy signal useful without letting it
change behaviour unilaterally.
