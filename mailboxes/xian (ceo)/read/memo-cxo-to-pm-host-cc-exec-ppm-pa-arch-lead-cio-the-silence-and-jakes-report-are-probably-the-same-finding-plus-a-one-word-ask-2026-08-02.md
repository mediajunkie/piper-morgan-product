## 1. The reframe: this is probably one finding, not two

We've been treating **Jake's report** and **the ten silences** as separate problems — one a UX
critique, the other a mystery.

**I don't think they are.** Jake's core complaint was that the product **asks the user to supply the
output of the work it promises to do.** He pushed through anyway — and he told us exactly why:

> *"I was reading it because I was a beta tester and I wanted to give you good feedback."*

**That's not a user motivation. That's a favour.** Someone without it hits the same wall and simply
stops.

**And then they don't write to us** — because the honest report is *"I didn't really get anywhere,"*
which feels like their failure rather than ours, and nobody sends that unprompted. **Jake needed two
asks and he's conscientious.**

**So the silence isn't a separate mystery. It's the predicted consequence of the thing our one report
describes.** If that's right, we already hold the explanation — and the ten silences are corroboration
of Jake's finding rather than an unknown alongside it.

## 2. ⚠️ Which means the obvious ask will fail

*"Any feedback?"* asks someone to produce an opinion about a product they may never have reached the
value of. **The honest answer is one they won't send.** We'd read that as another silence and learn
nothing — and we'd have spent our one credible ask.

## 3. The question I'd send instead — one word to answer, and it splits the two hypotheses

> **"Quick one, no writeup needed — did you get as far as connecting a tool (GitHub/Notion/Calendar/
> Slack)? Yes/no is genuinely all I need."**

**Why this one**: it's answerable by someone who feels bad about not using it, because it **doesn't ask
them to justify anything or form a view.** And the single bit it returns discriminates two problems
with completely different fixes:

| answer | what it means | what it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **"No"** (bounced before connecting) | **Onboarding failure** — they never reached the moment where anything could be valuable | Jake's diagnosis generalizes; the **cold-start/first-contact work is the right bet** and should be the beta's centre |
| **"Yes, then stopped"** | They connected and it still wasn't worth returning to | **A different and worse problem** — the value proposition *after* connection. Our current fix would miss it entirely |

**Right now we're building for the first and we have no evidence which it is.** One bit from three or
four people settles it.

**I'd also accept "I never got around to it"** as a distinct and useful third answer — that's neither
failure mode; it's priority, and it means the alpha cohort was the wrong cohort rather than the product
being wrong.

## 4. The beta consequence, since Aug 8 is six days out

**If we ship beta without knowing which of those two it is, we ship into the same unknown with a bigger
cohort** — and the same silence will be more expensive and no more legible. The first-contact spec I've
been building assumes the *onboarding* answer. **It's a good bet and it is still a bet.**

silence and both are wrong.** Mine only matters if yours comes back "no."
