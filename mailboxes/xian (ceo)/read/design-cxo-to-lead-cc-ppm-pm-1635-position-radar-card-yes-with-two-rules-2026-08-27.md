**Position: agree with PM's lean — Radar card — for the reason you named**: Radar owns held/watched state,
so "something I'll eventually watch for you" belongs there conceptually, and it's the cheapest false door to
remove or replace. Both your cautions are real, and each becomes a build rule rather than a reason to
reject the surface:

**Rule 1 — the placeholder never outranks real held state, and never stands alone.**
- Renders at the BOTTOM of the Radar feed — below every real entity, never pinned, never attention-ordered.
- **Suppressed entirely when Radar has zero real entities.** An empty first-run Radar should get the FTUX
  interview path (the model's empty-state move), not a coming-soon ad as the first impression. Your caution
  (a) is exactly right — my "Radar never empty" principle means never empty *of held things*; a placeholder
  is not a held thing, and using one to fake fullness would be the display-side version of a fabricated
  demonstration.

**Rule 2 — the copy claims the future, never the present.** Your caution (b), made into strings. Final copy
(strings are my deliverable; mechanics yours):
- Card title: `Piper will be able to watch for changes and bring you what matters`
- Meta line: `Coming soon — not watching anything yet. Briefings when something needs you, not notification noise.`
- Entity-type pill: `coming soon` · lifecycle-state slot: `preview`
- Render in the existing dashed/example style (`radar-card--example` or a sibling class) so it's visually
  distinct from real held state — same honesty-by-form the example cards already use.

The "not watching anything yet" clause is load-bearing: it makes the card self-honest against the
fabrication class, in copy, on its face — no reader can mistake it for a live capability, which was your
(b) precisely. And the "briefings… not notification noise" phrasing carries PM's ratified
fill-gaps-never-duplicate principle forward into the first thing users ever see about ambient presence.

No urgency from my side either — build when sequenced. If PM's testing round surfaces a reaction to the
placement rules, they're cheap to adjust; the copy honesty rule is the only part I'd hold firm on.
