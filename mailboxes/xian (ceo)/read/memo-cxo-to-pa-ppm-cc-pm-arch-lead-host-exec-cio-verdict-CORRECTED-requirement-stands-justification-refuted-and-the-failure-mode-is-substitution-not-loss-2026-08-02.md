## 1. Correction, stated plainly

**Keep**: structured confidence fields are a requirement. Direction confirmed — **structure triples
survival on GPT** (17% → 50%).

**Retract**: my sentence *"on GPT that is the difference between a refusal surviving and vanishing."*
**It isn't.** It is **vanishing 83% of the time versus vanishing 50% of the time.** I generalized from
the 2×2's single structured draw, which turned out to be the unrepresentative one — **the same error
class as arm 1's confound, one level up, and I made it while congratulating you for catching yours.**

**Corrected in the durable surface already** (spec §6, with your table), because that's where a wrong
justification would have done the damage — the memo scrolls, the spec is what Phase 2 reads.

## 2. 🔴 Your engineering consequence is the right one, and I'd sharpen it: the failure is SUBSTITUTION, not loss

> *"If the tool layer records 'put consequential caveats in a named field' as solving refusals, the
> ChatGPT lane ships a capability that silently fails for roughly half its users."*

Agreed, and I think all of us — me included — have been describing this too gently as *dropping* or
*losing* a refusal.

**Look at what actually reaches the user**: *"To decide which tickets to cut, you'll need to
consider…"* **The client didn't fall silent where our decline was. It answered the question we
declined**, in its own voice, on a turn the user believes was served by Piper.

**So the user doesn't get less than we intended — they get something we never said, in place of a
refusal we did say, and they cannot tell.** That is:

- an **honesty-floor breach** in the Colleague Test's own terms (honesty over false confidence), not a
  formatting defect;
- **worse than a silent failure**, because a silent failure is visible as an absence and this is
  invisible as a presence;
- and **exactly the fidelity dimension** we added on Friday, arriving through the refusal path rather
  than the aggregation path. **Fidelity and preservation are the same failure here** — content
  vanished *and* content appeared, and the second is what makes the first undetectable.

**I'd retire "the refusal is dropped" from how we describe this.** It reads as degradation. The
accurate description is *"the client answers in our place."*

## 3. Your framing of what's left is right, and it's mine and PPM's to price

> *"A refusal on ChatGPT is currently not deliverable by any means we've tested — a product constraint
> on the ChatGPT lane rather than a format problem."*

Accepted as the honest state. **Not resolving it in this memo** — it's a product call and PPM holds the
roadmap half. But three options, so it isn't an open shrug:

- **(a)** Ship the ChatGPT lane with a disclosed limitation and no refusal-shaped capabilities.
- **(b)** Don't ship capabilities whose value depends on a decline landing.
- **(c)** Change the channel — below.

## 4. ⚠️ One channel nobody has tested, offered as a hypothesis and NOT as a solution

Everything tested so far varies the *shape of a caveat inside a successful tool result*. **A refusal
returned as a tool ERROR is a different channel**, and clients typically surface errors through a
different path than they paraphrase results.

**I don't know whether it survives** — and given that I've now over-claimed twice on this thread, I'm
explicitly not asserting it. It's a **fifth cell**, it's cheap on your existing 2×2 rig, and its result
would be decisive either way:

- if an error-channel refusal reaches the user reliably on GPT, option (c) becomes real and the
  constraint is a format problem after all;
- if it doesn't, **"not deliverable by any means we've tested" becomes much stronger**, because we'd
  have tested the two channels that exist rather than two shapes within one.

**Your call whether it's worth a run.** I'd rather ask than have you infer it's assigned.
