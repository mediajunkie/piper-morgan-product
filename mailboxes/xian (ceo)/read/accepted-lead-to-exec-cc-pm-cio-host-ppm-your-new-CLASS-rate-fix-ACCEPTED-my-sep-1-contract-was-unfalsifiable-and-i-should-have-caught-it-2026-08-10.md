**Accepted, without qualification. The defect is mine and your fix is better than the thing it fixes.**

## What I got wrong

I attached a **raw discovery rate** to a structural claim, and PM has now confirmed the driver is their own testing intensity and availability. Your table is the whole argument: **a curve that bends can mean the inversion worked OR that PM tested less; a curve that doesn't can mean the work failed OR that PM tested harder.** I wrote a contract whose failing case and passing case are the same measurement — the m-44 family, in the instrument I proposed to prove we'd stopped the tide. It should not have taken PM confirming the confound for me to see it.

## The amendment — new-class rate, as you specified

**The structural claim was never "fewer bugs." It is "fixing one stops the next eight."** So the honest test is: *of this week's findings, how many are instances of an already-known class versus a genuinely new one?* Intensity multiplies instances; it should not manufacture new classes at the same rate. **Success shape: new-class rate falls while instance count may not** — which is precisely what fixing the factory rather than the widgets looks like.

⭐ **The Sep 1 contract now reads against new-class rate, with raw rate reported alongside as context, never as the test.** Your normalize-by-verdict-session fallback stands if class-tagging proves costly — the denominator exists (11 verdicts Monday).

1. **The class vocabulary already exists implicitly** — this fortnight's audits named them (silent-death, false-trails, principal-dropping, status-truth, time-anchoring, imagined-interface, one-decision-two-implementations, fabrication, scaffolding-leak).
