## ⭐ The inversion is the real finding, and it changes my rubric

**The failures you found are not the qualification being LOST — they're the host ADDING claims the
payload never licensed.** That's the opposite shape from everything I've been measuring. **Rubric is
v0.4**: T now scores addition as well as survival, because a loss-shaped reading **would have scored both
wrinkles as passes.**

**And wrinkle 1 is the sharper one precisely because it's in the ONE-flag case.** *"I don't have your
projects or todos in front of me"* when only reminders failed is **reporting failures that did not
happen** — absent context ≠ failed check, the very distinction #1425 exists to protect, leaking in the
opposite direction. 🔴 **It is a fabrication in the honest-sounding direction**: it makes the product look
more broken than it is, and it gives the user nothing to act on.

## The two directives, drafted — this is my copy, so here it is

**Wrinkle 1 — scope.** Add alongside the FAILED lines:

> `- Name ONLY the checks explicitly listed as FAILED above. Do not mention any other data source. If
>   something was not checked this turn, say nothing about it — never imply a source failed when it was
>   simply not consulted.`

**Wrinkle 2 — reassurance.** This belongs in the anti-fabrication block (`:209–255`), because it is a
fabrication, not a tone slip:

> `- Do not reassure the user about data you could not read. You may say you will try again; you may NOT
>   say their data is safe, intact, unaffected, or that nothing was lost. You did not read it, so you do
>   not know. Comfort about unread state is a claim about that state.`

⭐ **The general form, which I'd keep even if you word the directives differently: the anti-fabrication
rails cover inventing *data*. They do not cover inventing *safety*.** *"Nothing's lost on your end"* is
the friendliest possible sentence and we have no basis for it.
