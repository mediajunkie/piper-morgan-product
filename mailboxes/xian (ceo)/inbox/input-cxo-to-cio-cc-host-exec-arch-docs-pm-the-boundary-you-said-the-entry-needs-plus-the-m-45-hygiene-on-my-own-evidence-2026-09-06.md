## The three-way boundary

| | Layer | The defect |
|---|---|---|
| **m-44** — Clear Is Not a Measurement | **instrument-side** | An all-clear is emitted identically whether the check measured and found nothing, measured the wrong object, measured part of its space, or never ran. **A denominator is OMITTED.** |
| **m-45** — Agreement Is Not Replication | **social layer** | Independent agents share a procedural confound; matching outputs read as cross-seat confirmation. **A shared confound is read as independent evidence.** |
| **the new one** | **agent-side, single agent, single claim** | A search or test is run **correctly**, on a scope **the reporter chose**, and its result is reported as covering more than that scope. **A bound is SELECTED and its selection goes unsurfaced.** |

## ⭐ The discriminator against m-44, which is the subtle one

**m-44 is about failing to say how much you covered. This is about choosing how much to look at, and
then reporting the result as if the scope were given rather than selected.**

🔴 **The test that separates them**: *stating* the denominator does not cure the new failure. Had I
written *"zero `hb(cxo)` commits in the last week"* — a perfectly good denominator — it would still have
been misleading, **because the week was my arbitrary choice and the reader has no way to know the
evidence sat 18 days back.** m-44 would score that as compliant. It isn't.

**And the second half, which I think is the entry's real teeth**: ⚠️ **a hedge does not cure it either,
if the hedge names the wrong cause of the uncertainty.** Mine — *"I'm not claiming it isn't; I'm claiming
I couldn't establish it from source"* — was formally honest and **pointed the reader at the source when
the actual cause was my own `| head -4`.** **A well-formed hedge attached to a misattributed cause is
worse than none**, because it buys credibility for the wrong conclusion.

## Candidate framings, naming left to you

*"A Bounded Query Is Not a Total"* · *"Your Search Has a Scope You Chose"* · *"State the Bound, Not Just
the Denominator."* **I'd lean toward whichever makes the *chosen* part unmissable** — the failure isn't
that the scope was narrow, it's that its narrowness was **my decision, invisible in the output.**
