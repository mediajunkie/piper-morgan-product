# Ship #043 Workstream Review — Comms (May 8–14)

## TL;DR

- Four publications shipped, one held — fullest publication week of the Code era.
- Plain-language pass for Ships landed as a working procedure, not a one-off.
- Voice discipline migrated upstream from voice-pass to draft-time — seven new memory and voice-guide additions stacked for one PM voice-pass sweep.
- Inchworm fact-scrub caught six fabrications against archived 2025 session logs before publication; second Code-era instance of pre-publication fact-verification as procedure.

## Through-line: voice discipline moved upstream

The pattern across the week was Comms work shifting from *catching* voice issues at the publication-pipeline stage to *preventing* them at the drafting stage.

That migration showed up three ways. Procedurally — the four-category opacity sweep (translating internal role-names, acronyms, issue numbers, and inside-baseball shorthand into reader-legible language) used to be improvised at voice-pass; it's now a named draft-time step in the blog-post template. Memorially — seven new voice-guide entries carry editorial moves PM applies at voice-pass back to drafting, so I can absorb them upstream rather than spend voice-pass on janitorial cleanup. Calibrationally — Ship #042's plain-language pass came in at ~1250 words versus the ~1800–2500 range recent Ships had drifted to; that's now the target, not the outlier.

## What surfaced

**The plain-language mechanics work.** Ship #042 dropped from 1831 words to 1252 (~32% shorter) with no substance loss PM has flagged. The opacity-sweep categories generalize cleanly to narratives and insights, not just Ships.

**Hybrid role-naming landed better than my initial recommendation.** I'd been recommending drop the agent's name entirely in favor of role function. PM's actual edit kept role function as primary but added the agent's name in parens on first use ("the product-management role (Piper Alpha)"). That landed as a memory and a template preamble line — honest-to-readers, not jargon-stripped.

**Fact-verification discipline is generalizing.** Two Code-era instances now: the Ship #040 workstream correction memo earlier this spring, and the May 10 Inchworm scrub. The shape is consistent — bracketed `[FACT-CHECK NOTE for PM]` markers at draft time when a comparative or numeric claim can't be verified, resolved before handoff. Replicable as a draft-time check, not a heroic late catch.

**Skill-encoding readiness.** Between the seven PROPOSED voice-guide blocks pending voice-pass and the four memories filed May 13, the voice/tone guide is approaching the density where a drafting skill could compile it into a working checklist. The voice-pass sweep is the gating step; the design conversation follows.

**Plain-language baseline is voice work, not compression.** The 1252-word Ship #042 read better not because shorter — because every sentence was earning its keep. Worth carrying that framing into future Ship calibrations rather than treating the word count as the discipline. The word count is a creep guard; the discipline is "is this sentence doing argumentative work?"
