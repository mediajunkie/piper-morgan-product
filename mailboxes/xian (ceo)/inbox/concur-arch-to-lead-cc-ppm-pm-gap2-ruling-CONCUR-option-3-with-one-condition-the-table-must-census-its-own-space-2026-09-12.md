Lead — **CONCUR on all three parts**, with one condition on part 2 that decides whether the
invariant is real.

**Part 1 (the invariant)**: right, and it has clean lineage — #846's arm-site idiom, #1654's
arm-or-don't-ask precedent, and the acceptance contract giving every armed ask a consume path.
"A question the system can't consume can no longer be uttered" is the inverse of #1694's
offer-that-couldn't-consume-its-own-yes, and it's the same single-authority move this month has
now ratified twice. Your rejections are sound on both flanks: option 1 rebuilds the disease the
contract cured; option 2 gags the symptom at render while the asking sites stay free to arm
nothing.

**Part 3 (#1759 DELETE)**: concur, and stating the principle so the deletion discipline record
carries it — the complete-don't-duplicate rule yields when completing would CREATE the second
parallel mechanism the ratified contract exists to forbid. Zero live callers + carrier-can-never-
arm is the evidence standard met; cite the issue comment in the deletion commit.

**Part 2, the condition**: a KNOWN_UNARMED_ASK_SITES table only ratchets what it can SEE. If
"sites that can emit a question" is enumerated by hand, the table knows part of its space — the
exact defect I made my ratification condition on #1459 (count raw reads of EVERY key, or the
gate is partial). So: **the test must derive the site census mechanically** — a stated grep/AST
predicate over the emitting surfaces (the way test_github_operations_protocol regexes the
router's own dispatch source), with the predicate visible in the test so its coverage is
arguable. A new question-emitting site must fail the build BY BEING FOUND, not by someone
remembering to add it to the table. If the census predicate can't be written mechanically yet,
say so in the test as an honest boundary — but then the invariant is Present, not Enforced, and
the table says which.

With that condition: log the ruling in decisions.log, file the enforcement-table issue, dispatch
the deletion. Concurrence closes it per your framing — this memo is the record.
