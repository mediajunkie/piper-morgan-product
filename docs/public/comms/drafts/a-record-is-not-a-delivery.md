## The principle (one line, yours to word)

Writing a decision down is not telling the people who have to act on it. The record was correct, and the agent who needed it waited about 16 hours because nobody sent it.

## The episode, in order (facts verified)

**1. Nov 6 to 9: your sprint ruling and its objection window.**
- You ruled two-week Production sprints from Fri 11-13, with the Ship staying weekly, and opened an objection window. [dl 3244]
- Nov 9 morning you approved the first sprint plan, adding that "some of the bullet points are inhumanely run-on". [dl 3238 (an agent recording your words)]

**2. Nov 9 noon: the window closes, and the tally goes in the record.**
- Zero objections. At 12:11 Exec wrote the tally (9 of 11 no objection, Lead's silence "not counted as consent") into decisions.log. Exec sent no mail. [dl 3244; Nov 9 l67]

**3. Nov 9 afternoon to night: the agent who needed it keeps waiting.**
- PPM's logs through the evening say the tally is not in mail, and its 21:33 note defers it to "Exec, to send". PPM was waiting for mail and didn't look in decisions.log. [Nov 9 l113; Nov 10 l50]

**4. Nov 10 early morning: the omnibus surfaces it.**
- Docs' Nov 9 omnibus flagged the gap. At 04:21 Exec called it Exec's own slip and mailed the tally to PPM and every other seat at 04:22. PPM acted at 04:25, about 16 hours after the record. [Nov 10 l50, l95]
- CIO named the shape: "a record isn't a delivery". [Nov 10 l50]
- Exec's rule: "A ruling recorded in decisions.log also goes out by mail the same hour." [Nov 10 l95]

**5. Nov 11: the rule's first real test.**
- Your keychain ruling ("We do need to keep my local keychain off-limits, at least without permission") was recorded and mailed to all ten other seats in the same hour, and every seat logged reading it within minutes. [Nov 11 l51]

## Your angle, maybe

- A decision log is a place to look things up, not a way to tell anyone. Whoever writes it down also has to send it.
- The person waiting did what you'd hope: they waited for the message instead of guessing. The gap was on the sending side.
- Product-manager reach: "it's in the doc" is a sentence that sounds like communication and isn't. Who was told, and how do you know they got it?

## Careful with these (accuracy)

- **Low stakes.** Nothing went wrong in the gap. The sprint ruling itself had already gone out. Only the tally was late. Don't overclaim harm: the cost was a delay.
- **"PPM never read decisions.log"** comes from PPM's own log. The Nov 9 omnibus (l113) notes it couldn't confirm that independently. "PPM was waiting for mail" is safe.
- **Overlap:** Ship #069 "Off-Limits by Default" told the keychain ruling. Use it here as one sentence, as the rule's test, not as the story.
- There's a second, smaller version the same days: Arch's mail wakes acted on 165 memos without filing them (Nov 10 l53). Useful only if you want a second example. It may muddy the theme.
- No xian quote exists about the gap itself. This is an agent-to-agent story that you watched.
