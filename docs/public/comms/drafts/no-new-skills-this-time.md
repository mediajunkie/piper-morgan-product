# No New Skills This Time

*October 23 – November 6, 2026*

## The principle in one sentence

When a team reviews what it keeps wishing it had, the honest answer is usually a missing line in something that already exists, not a new procedure, and a review that builds nothing has still done its job.

## Beats, in order (facts verified, source in brackets)

**1. Why the review exists.** In July you noticed "emergent self-improvement and self-healing behaviors. Some from the model upgrades perhaps but others from our own harness." The monthly skills review was set up as the channel for that: triage what the system is already teaching itself. You ratified the monthly cadence on Jul 8: "yes monthly seems right." [skill-candidates-review.md l.3, l.5]

**2. It was built to say no.** The review has three columns: build, fold into something existing, and don't build. The don't-build column "exists because of PM's don't-overlearn principle": "a process with a long clean track record that failed once under compound exogenous disruption usually needs repair-readiness, not new machinery." [l.11, l.24-29]

**3. Then it didn't run.** "The review never ran in September, and that silently skipped October." CIO's fix (10-23) makes a skipped cycle visible: the reminder issue now gets a comment when a cycle comes due and is skipped. Your ruling that day: pick up the missed cycle now and put it back on schedule. [l.49, decisions.log l.2776] Possible angle: the review of the system's self-repair habits needed repairing itself.

**4. What the team said it wanted.** Exec gathered candidates from Sep 1 to Oct 23: "Wanted but not found" lines in session logs, the omnibus logs, and its own view across the team. Ten new candidates plus three from July, 13 in all. Some examples that read well to an outsider: [l.49, l.56-67]
- Environment gotchas (no venv in the agents' worktrees, macOS `sed`, zsh not splitting a variable). The broadest signal: 6 roles or more, every week. Ruled: don't build a skill, write reference entries.
- A read-only way to query alpha or production (user counts, unused invites). About 10 logs across 6 roles. Ruled: not a skill, it's a tool-and-permission decision.
- A production observation seat. CXO asked 8 days running in September. Ruled: don't build.
- Record your ruling the same hour, and check a relay landed. Ruled: one prompt line in the mail-checking skill.

**5. The answer.** You put it off on 10-23 to do it "tomorrow, when he's fresh" (Exec's words) and disposed of it on Nov 6 by list, not a walk-through: "I accept the 12 leans." Result: build 0, fold 5, don't build 6, closed as already done 2. [decisions.log l.2782, l.3129, review l.72-73]

**6. The finding.** "Nothing became a skill. Most signal pointed at a missing line in an existing place, or at a tool or permission, not a procedure." And two of Exec's leans were already stale by review day, because the system had moved on (seats now wake from a scheduler, not session timers, and a push script already existed). CIO caught both by checking against the actual files before you saw them. [l.74, l.63, l.66]

**7. It's back on schedule.** Next cycle due Tue Dec 1. [l.74]

## Careful with these (accuracy)
- **13 candidates, but "12 leans".** 13 were reviewed (10 new plus 3 from July). You accepted 12 leans on Nov 6. The 13th (the skill-scope audit) was already ruled and done on Nov 4 on your "Yes finish it". Say "thirteen ideas" for the count, or "a dozen" loosely. Don't say "twelve candidates".
- "Folded" items did get built, as small edits (a line in a briefing, a step in a runbook). The claim is no *new skill*, not no change.
- "Every week" and "8 days running" are counts of log files that mention a topic, which "overstate chatty topics" (l.49).
- The skill count: 34 skills with a SKILL.md (counted 11-06). The "36" in the doc's 10-23 gather was wrong.

## Your angle, maybe
- Agents (like people) ask for new process when a sentence in the right place would do.
- The value of a review is in the don't-build column. A review that adds nothing keeps the system small.
- Wanting a skill vs. wanting permission: several "skills" were really requests for access or a seat.
