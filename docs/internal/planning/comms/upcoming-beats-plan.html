The categorization question — sharper than "is Era 2 getting broad"

What I said 8 Aug: Era 2 (“blog-first + Code-era, Mar 2026 onward”) has absorbed 107 posts over six months and probably needs a split, with the seam at the move to the always-on host in late July.

What I actually found when I went and checked the site today: it's not a soft "getting broad" problem. The site's visitor-facing Eras browse feature (src/lib/episodes.ts in the website repo) defines exactly five eras, and the fifth and last one ends 2026-03-31. There is no era after that, anywhere in the code — not a broad one, none. It's a live, rendered feature (the blog index's era filter), not dead metadata: I confirmed it's actually imported and used in BlogContent.tsx.

FactNumber
Posts published since Era 5 ended (2026-03-31)94
Span with zero era coverage2026-04-01 → 2026-08-11, 4.5 months
What that span containsThe entire Claude Code migration, alpha launch, the always-on-host move, and literally everything in this document

So this isn't "should we split Era 2 for tidiness." It's "a visitor-facing navigation feature has been silently frozen for nearly five months," and every beat in this document — 24 through 37 — would publish into that same uncategorized gap. Worth deciding before, not after, another dozen posts land there.

My 8 Aug seam argument still seems right to me on reflection, and now has a name worth testing: if the theme that's recurred independently across two separate sweeps this month really is the season where the team turned on its own instruments, that could plausibly be Era 6, dated from the always-on-host move (late July) rather than a generic continuation of "Era 2: everything since March." That would turn today's "which beats do we run" conversation and the "how do we categorize" conversation into the same decision, which is probably worth naming out loud before either gets decided separately.

One thing I deliberately didn't do: propose new era names or boundaries beyond that one seam. Era-naming isn't mine to freelance — that's exactly the kind of curatorial call this document exists to bring to you, not make for you.
