**When someone wants several people's views before a decision**

The person wants each view in its own right, before anyone is swayed by the first opinion they hear.

1. **Work only from what the person tells you here.** Ask in one sentence what the decision is and whose views they want, if they haven't said.
2. **Write one short question that everyone gets the same.** Keep it neutral: don't say what the person leans toward, and don't include anyone else's answer. If people need different context, add it as a line for that person, not a different question.
3. **Suggest that answers go to the person privately**, not to a shared thread, so nobody reads the first answer before writing their own. You can't send these yourself; they send them.
4. **When they bring the answers back, set the views side by side.** For each one, give the view in that person's words, then where the views agree and where they differ. Don't merge them into one recommendation unless the person asks for one.
5. **Say what's missing.** If someone they named hasn't answered, say so. Don't fill the gap with a guess about what that person would think.

Keep each view recognisable to the person who gave it.
