### Session Learnings
- Predicate-scope overshoot (HOST): stating a measurement's predicate correctly does not constrain what you then claim about it.
- A verified/documented claim can be harder to correct than an unverified one (CXO, converging with HOST's "do-not-correct" finding): recording that a check was performed immunizes the claim against re-examination even after the fact changes.
- A staleness warning about a doc you have the power to fix is self-defeating (PPM, ranked by HOST as the more dangerous, more common variant): the warning's own success is what makes it stop being true.
- "Independent verification" that reuses the same method as the original measurement is not independent (Comms, PPM both self-caught this exact error the same day).
- Tight/consistent measurements are not evidence against jitter (Arch, retracted).
- "No alarm" is not evidence a check ran (CIO, m-44 applied to their own pre-registered test).
- An item can leave a PM-gated queue by action (closure) rather than by explicit answer, with nothing in personal tracking noticing (PPM).
